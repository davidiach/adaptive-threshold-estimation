AUDIT OF THE 29 OCTOBER 2026 MANUSCRIPT REVISION

Disposition
-----------
No substantive error or counterexample was found in the changed mathematical
bounds, new examples, or genie-convergence repair. The audit found one minor
endpoint wording regression and one editorial splice. Neither affects the
theorem statements or requires changing any of the new constants.

This audit examines the changes against the original, follows the numerical
constants through their dependencies, and uses an independent checker. It does
not reuse or import checks/verify_revisions.py. The revised manuscript, PDF,
and source ZIP have not been modified by this audit.

1. Actual introduced defects, both minor
--------------------------------------
1a. sections/matching_lower.tex:259, PDF p.15.
    "Every retained component is supported below 1/128" should allow equality.
    A concrete literal counterexample is mu = delta_(1/128), rho = 1/4,
    h = 1/4096 (B = 2^24). Retaining the single band costs 1/64; treating it
    ordinarily costs 1/32. It is therefore retained. Its group has n=1, so
    no trimming occurs, and the resulting component has all its mass exactly
    at 1/128. Replace the phrase by "supported on (0,1/128]". Proposition B.4
    already permits b_1 <= 1/128, so the proof and result remain valid.

1b. sections/transfer_foundations.tex:547-555, PDF p.36.
    The inserted normalization explanation interrupts "Consequently,
    writing ..., we obtain" with "The conversion uses ...". This is a
    grammatical splice, not a normalization error. Introduce the normalized
    quantities with a complete sentence, display the identity, and put
    "Consequently," immediately before the resulting lower bound.

The companion revised_changes_editorial.patch proposes exactly these two
changes and has not been applied to the delivered manuscript.

2. Sharper constants: analytic checks
------------------------------------
Ordinary-band mass. For a candidate added coordinate x, the entropy increase
is Delta = x + A log(1+x/A) + x log(1+A/x). Since log(1+t) <= sqrt(t),
Delta <= x + 2 sqrt(Ax) <= x + 2 sqrt(Fx). Thus
sqrt(F+Delta)-sqrt(F) <= sqrt(x). Inner optimality gives
x b_j <= sqrt(rho x), hence w_j = x b_j^2 <= rho. The F=0 case also works.
The argument continues to require an INNER minimizer, as the revision states.

Dyadic factor two. If the selected mass at the cutoff value f is r, the
selected integral is at least (2s-r)f. Excluded smaller values contribute at
most sf, and the excluded boundary mass at most (s-r)f. Thus excluded integral
is at most selected integral. For n equal masses s at consecutive dyadic
values, total integral divided by the best mass-s integral is 2-2^(1-n).
Consequently the factor two is sharp for this standalone dyadic inequality.
The replacements h H_O <= 2 Psi and 10 Psi -> 3 Psi are consistent.

Weighted entropy. The stronger floor c_bar >= 3/40 and the original overlap
bound log Z <= 8+log(25/3) give
  1+Ent(pi) <= [65+(40/3)(9+log(25/3))] c_bar.
The bracket is approximately 213.270180482668, strictly below 214.
Downstream factors are 4*214=856 and 856*1040=890240. After the moment-to-
variance factor 3/2, the required denominator is 1,335,360, below the retained
2,000,000. The other two annular branches require only 8192 and 864 after
factoring out exp(28), so they are also covered.

Explicit upper constant. Tracking the shifted Poisson sum, the ordinary
kernel, mixed term, and unresolved-mass terms gives coefficients
  entropy: 97.5975867152431,
  ordinary: 25206.5936167890,
and the bias coefficient 128. These are below the displayed rounded bounds
104, 27000, and the final 33000. If z > rho/128, the constant estimator has
risk rho/2 < 64z, which is also covered. Taking the outer cap by rho causes
no additional multiplicative loss.

Explicit lower constant. Retaining the bias b_i=r_i/64 and localization
factor 1/128 gives the actual packed contribution
  (sum_i G_i)/128 * min{lambda^2/65536,
                       sqrt(c_ann)*lambda/(32 sqrt(B))}.
Using sum_i G_i >= F/896 and lambda^2=rho/F permits
  c_E = sqrt(c_ann)/(896*128*32) = approximately 1.60211725515501e-16.
This is smaller than the coefficient of the first branch. The high-gap
comparison is at most 64 sqrt(1+log 6) = approximately 106.9347782 < 112.
With R >= Psi/8192, the final 3 Psi assembly, and K <= 2k, the common lower
coefficient is [2(24576+1/c_E)]^-1 = approximately 8.01058627574351e-17.
The printed 1e-17 is therefore conservative. An exact elementary check is
  2(24576+896*128*32*1500*3^14) = 52,660,718,272,561,152 < 10^17,
using exp(1)<3 and sqrt(2,000,000)<1500. The z>=rho branch and F=0 branch
are separately covered by the Psi bound.

3. New examples and optimizer
----------------------------
The noninterval example was recomputed independently at 100-digit precision
using G(a)=A(1+log A)-sum a_j log a_j. All eight printed intervals contain
the corresponding values. The unique best mask is {1/2,1/8}, with cost/h
3.22748280880832; the runner-up is all three atoms, with cost/h
3.22943894432090. The gap is approximately 0.00195613551258337 per unit h.
Every vertex cost is below the outer cap. Concavity shows that a unique
minimizing vertex also rules out a different fractional minimizer: any
nontrivial convex combination involving another vertex has strictly larger
averaged vertex cost. An additional 2,197 fractional assignments were tested.

For mu{2^-j}=1/[j(j+1)], the tail sum is exactly 1/(J+1). Split the ordinary
cost at J/2: its first part is bounded by 2^(-floor(J/2)) and its second part
by 8/J^2. Both full inverse moments diverge. Once 1/(J+1)<=rho, the indicator
allocation on j>J is feasible for Psi and has objective exactly the tail
mass, since h/d>1 there. The stated matching scales are valid for fixed rho.

The common Psi optimizer also survives. For h<alpha, lambda=h/(2alpha)
supports phi_d(w)=min{w,(h/d)sqrt(w)} at w_*(d). At d>alpha the derivative
equals lambda; at h<d<=alpha the left derivative at w=1 is at least lambda;
at d<=h, phi_d(w)=w. Integrating the supporting inequality and the mass
constraint proves optimality. For h>=alpha, phi_d(w_*)=w_* and the value is
rho. The mass equation is continuous and strictly increasing below one,
including for atomic laws. Independent scalar-dual minimization agreed on
100 numerical laws, including rarities above 1/4 as allowed by the proposition.

The new sharp-4b counterexample is valid on the stated HALF-prior class:
q(d)<=1/2 makes its displayed bias range [-b,b]. Its b also satisfies
b<=rho/64. With h=d, allocating weight one to the hard atom gives Psi>=epsilon.
For every fixed c<4, epsilon-cb tends to epsilon(1-c/4)>0, while the proposed
modified denominator is proportional to d^2. This defeats any fixed smaller
coefficient, despite variance-work at most 1/4.

4. Genie fix and new infinite-divergence example
-----------------------------------------------
All four priors in the example are admissible: theta(q_v)=theta(q_H)=
theta(q_w)=1/4. The latent divergence is
  C_v = (1/8) int_0^(1/2) [1/(1/4-d^2)] dd = infinity.
At cutoff d=1/2-2^-m its partial value is log(2^m-1)/8, so the divergence
is explicit. Reaching the genie at a fixed finite cap has infinite
information and finite work N.

The finite bound for ordinary policies is also correct. With u_s the
uniform-p prefix mass, direct beta integrals give
  |p_H(s)-p_L(s)| <= u_s/2,
  p_v(s) = 2(n-k+1)u_s/(n+2),
  r_s <= 3n u_s/8,
  p_w(s) >= u_s/2.
Summing over stopped ordered prefixes and the independent seed gives
information <= (3/8) E_U T <= (3/4) E_w T, hence K_infinity<=3/4.
The summation counts elapsed local work n; no missing binomial coefficient
is needed for ordered prefixes. The independent checker verified 2,144
prefix inequalities exactly and Bellman upper certificates through cap 64.

The conditional pointwise theorem uses C_v<infinity to obtain an L2 score.
Erasing a reveal loses at most e_N^v in information. Root-active work is
at least one, so the same bound controls the ratio difference. The full
Bernoulli stream identifies (D,S) because D>0; L2 martingale convergence
therefore gives e_N^v -> 0. No unjustified infinite-minus-infinite expression
is introduced in this finite-divergence assertion.

For optimized convergence, q_v^epsilon=(1-epsilon)q_v+epsilon(q_L+q_H)/2
remains admissible, bounds the latent score by 2/epsilon, and gives
K_infinity^epsilon <= K_infinity/(1-epsilon). Taking N to infinity at fixed
epsilon, then epsilon to zero, proves the required lower limit. It does
not require uniform pointwise convergence or exchange those limits.

The necessity of that order can be made quantitative in this same example:
  C_v^epsilon = [8(1-epsilon)]^-1
      log( ((4-3epsilon)/(4+3epsilon)) * ((8-epsilon)/epsilon) ).
For every fixed N, K_N^{+,epsilon} >= C_v^epsilon/N diverges as epsilon
decreases to zero, while K_infinity^epsilon <= 3/[4(1-epsilon)] remains
bounded. The revision handles precisely this obstruction.
