\subsection{RMS consequences of the obstruction and mixture bounds}
\label{sec:rms-localization}

The same common pairs give Lean proofs of the manuscript's generic RMS
transfer, \eqref{eq:bias-population-transfer} of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bias-transfer}, and of its additive submeasure version,
\eqref{eq:additive-localization} of
Theorem~\ref{thm:additive-localization}.
Theorems~\ref{thm:rms-obstruction-verified}
and~\ref{thm:rms-additive-verified} restate those bounds under equivalent
hypotheses and with the same coefficient $1/128$. The formal proofs take
the common pair from Theorem~\ref{thm:common-pair-verified}. They combine
components through the original-model construction of
Section~\ref{sec:additive-confidence}, not an oracle experiment with
visible labels, and they replace the total-variation testing step by an
affinity bound.

\begin{theorem}[Verified RMS obstruction transfer]
\label{thm:rms-obstruction-verified}
Under the variance--null-work premise of
Theorem~\ref{thm:common-pair-verified}, for every $B>0$,
\[
 R_{\mu,\rho}(B)\ge
 \frac1{128}\min\{b,\sqrt{J/B}\}.
\]
The bound holds for each observable estimator with uniform expected
work at most $B$, and for the minimax infimum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose the policy-uniform pair at separation
$s=\min\{b,\sqrt{J/B}\}/32$. Its local squared Hellinger bound
is $\kappa\mathbb E_0T$, where
$\kappa=42s^2/J\le42/(1024B)$.
The finite adaptive population decomposition charges each local
loss to the same midpoint population. Null work is at most twice
midpoint work, so the finite-prefix population laws satisfy
\[
 1-\Aff(P_{q,n},P_{r,n})\le2\kappa B
 \le\frac{21}{256}<\frac1{10}.
\]
The proof first restricts each finite decision tree to its finitely
many labels, then integrates over the arbitrary private seed.
Finite expected work at each endpoint and stopped-prefix
reconstruction extend the bound to every terminal binary decision,
as checked by the
\href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/8ed87544c861eee47a7b9fd2ede9483529b60e52/lean/ThresholdHC/RMSLocalization/Terminal.lean}{formal transfer}.

Let $a<b$ be the two target means. Threshold the estimator at
$(a+b)/2$. If both squared risks were below $(b-a)^2/16$,
Markov's inequality would make each testing error at most $1/4$.
For the resulting binary laws, write
$p=P_q\{1\}$ and $u=P_r\{0\}$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \Aff(P_q,P_r)
 =\sqrt{(1-p)u}+\sqrt{p(1-u)}
 \le\sqrt{(2-p-u)(p+u)}\le\frac78.
\]
This contradicts affinity at least $9/10$. Thus the maximum
RMS risk is at least $(b-a)/4\ge s/4$, proving the coefficient
$1/128$. The
\href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/8ed87544c861eee47a7b9fd2ede9483529b60e52/lean/ThresholdHC/RMSLocalization/CommonPair.lean}{generic RMS theorem}
constructs the common pair from the obstruction internally.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Verified additive RMS localization]
\label{thm:rms-additive-verified}
For the finite submeasures, normalized component laws, rarity
allocation and variance--null-work obstructions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:additive-confidence-verified}, for every $B>0$,
\[
 R_{\mu,\rho}(B)\ge\frac1{128}\sum_i\tau_i
       \min\{b_i,\sqrt{J_i/B}\}.
\]
The component supports may overlap.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $s_i=\min\{b_i,\sqrt{J_i/B}\}/32$ in each component.
The combined measurable priors constructed in
Section~\ref{sec:additive-confidence} have target difference at
least $\sum_i\tau_i s_i$ and the uniform local bound
$\kappa=42/(1024B)$ against original null work.
The preceding midpoint transfer and binary-testing argument give
RMS risk at least $\sum_i\tau_i s_i/4$.
A zero total contribution gives the zero bound.
The
\href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/8ed87544c861eee47a7b9fd2ede9483529b60e52/lean/ThresholdHC/RMSLocalization/Full.lean}{formal submeasure conclusions}
cover each admissible estimator and the minimax infimum.
\end{proof}
