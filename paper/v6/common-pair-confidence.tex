\subsection{Common pairs from a variance--null-work obstruction}
\label{sec:common-pair-confidence}

The common pair needed by the confidence transfer can now be
constructed from the stated single-coin obstruction. The argument
includes laws with an atom at gap $1/2$.

\begin{theorem}[Verified quantitative common pair]
\label{thm:common-pair-verified}
Fix a known probability law $\mu$ on $(0,1/2]$ and $0<\rho\le1/4$.
Suppose that every capped observable one-coin estimator with bounded
output, uniform bias at most $b>0$ on $X_\rho$, and uniform variance at
most $V$ satisfies
\[
 V\,\mathbb E_0T\ge J,\qquad J>0.
\]
For every $0<\gamma\le b/3$, there are $q,r\in X_\rho$ with
$\theta(r)-\theta(q)\ge\gamma$ such that, simultaneously for every
capped local procedure with independent auxiliary randomness,
\[
 1-\Aff(P_q^A,P_r^A)\le
 \frac{42\gamma^2}{J}\,\mathbb E_0T_A.
\]
The pair is chosen before the local policy, auxiliary seed and cap.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The zero-work constant estimator $\rho/2$ forces $b<\rho/2$.
For a fixed capped acquisition rule, its full canonical transcript has
the same Hellinger affinity as the finite statistic consisting of the
stopping count and number of heads. This identity holds even when
some category probabilities vanish. Indeed, for any common dominating
measure $\xi$,
\[
 \Aff(P,Q)=\int\sqrt{\frac{dP}{d\xi}\frac{dQ}{d\xi}}\,d\xi.
\]
The density depends on the transcript only through those two counts,
so the factor can be integrated over each statistic fiber. The
\href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/8cf32f7e4092929d46eb87ecbb67b1e231bbcaec/lean/ThresholdHC/ConfidenceLocalization/StatisticGeometry.lean}{finite-statistic identity}
also gives continuous convex Hellinger constraints on the compact
dual population-pair set.

Use the constant prior $c=\rho$ and restrict attention to
$q'=(q+c)/2$, $q\in X_\rho$. If $P_q$ denotes the categorical law,
then $cP_q\le P_c$ and $P_{q'}=(P_q+P_c)/2$. Thus all these
regularized laws have the same support and are strictly positive
there. The finite categorical convex comparison applies on this
support without any interior-gap hypothesis.

If every pair with $n$-fold affinity at least $1/2$ has target
separation less than $2\varepsilon$, that comparison and symmetric
one-run reduction give a finite score $Y$ with bias at most
$3\varepsilon$ and variance at most $9n\varepsilon^2$ on the regularized
class. Put
\[
 Z=Y+(\mathbb E_cY-c).
\]
Affineness of the one-coin law gives
$\mathbb E_qZ=2\mathbb E_{q'}Y-c$ and
$\theta(q)=2\theta(q')-c$. Mixture variance gives
$\Var_qZ=\Var_qY\le2\Var_{q'}Y$. Hence $Z$ has bias at most
$6\varepsilon$ and variance at most $18n\varepsilon^2$ on the entire
original class. Its actual stopping rule and work are unchanged.
This is the
\href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/8cf32f7e4092929d46eb87ecbb67b1e231bbcaec/lean/ThresholdHC/ConfidenceLocalization/CappedEstimator.lean}{checked bounded estimator construction}.

Write $w=\mathbb E_0T_A$ and set $\varepsilon=\gamma/2$.
Since $6\varepsilon\le b$, the obstruction implies that whenever
\[
 n\,\frac92\gamma^2w<J,
\]
there is a regularized pair with separation at least $\gamma$ and
$n$-fold affinity at least $1/2$.

On the compact convex set of raw prior pairs with separation at least
$2\gamma$, let $h$ be the regularized squared Hellinger distance.
It is continuous and takes values in $[0,1]$. If $h_*$ is its minimum,
choose an integer $n$ with $2\le nh_*\le3$ when $h_*>0$.
The elementary implication
\[
 (1-h)^n\ge\tfrac12 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad nh\le1
\]
shows that the preceding power-pair conclusion forces
\[
 J h_*\le\frac{27}{2}\gamma^2w\le42\gamma^2w.
\]
The same compact argument covers $w=0$ without dividing by work.

For each capped policy $A$, its constraint
$J h_A-42\gamma^2w_A$ is continuous and convex on the same compact
pair set. Every finite revealed mixture of such policies is an actual
capped policy. The chosen index is part of its independent seed,
and both affinity and null work are exactly the corresponding
weighted averages. Thus every finite convex mixture of constraints
has a feasible pair. Convex minimax gives simultaneous feasibility
for each finite family, and compactness gives one pair for all
policies and caps. Finally replace the raw priors by their half
mixtures with $c$. Their target gap is at least $\gamma$, and they
remain in $X_\rho$. This proves the
\href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/8cf32f7e4092929d46eb87ecbb67b1e231bbcaec/lean/ThresholdHC/ConfidenceLocalization/CommonPair.lean}{policy-uniform common-pair theorem}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Verified confidence consequence]
\label{cor:common-pair-confidence}
Under the same variance--null-work obstruction, for $B>0$ and
$0<\delta\le1/4$,
\[
 r_{\rm exp}(B,\delta)\ge
 \frac1{128}\min\left\{b,\sqrt{\frac{J\log(1/\delta)}{B}}\right\}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Set $\gamma=\min\{b,\sqrt{J\log(1/\delta)/B}\}/32$.
The common pair just constructed satisfies the local premise of the
stopped $16/3$ KL transfer. Quarter smoothing halves its separation.
Midpoint testing would require KL at least
$\log(1/\delta)/4$, while the transfer bounds it by
$7\log(1/\delta)/32$ if the radius were smaller than the displayed
lower bound. The
\href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/8cf32f7e4092929d46eb87ecbb67b1e231bbcaec/lean/ThresholdHC/ConfidenceLocalization/ConfidenceRate.lean}{formal conclusion}
covers actual observable estimators and the minimax infimum.
\end{proof}
