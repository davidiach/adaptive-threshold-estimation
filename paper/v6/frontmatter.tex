\begin{abstract}
A population of coins has unknown heads probabilities $P$, and we want the
fraction $\theta=\Prob(P>1/2)$ that favour heads. Each flip costs one unit.
An algorithm may create coins, return to old ones and interleave them
adaptively, with an expected budget of $B$ flips. Let $\mu$ be the law of
the gap $D=|P-1/2|$ on $(0,1/2]$. At each fixed $\mu$ we determine the
minimax root-mean-square error, where the worst case is over every way the
sign of $P-1/2$ can depend on the gap: it lies within universal constant
factors of $\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2})$, where $\omega_\mu(h)=\E_\mu\min\{1,h/D\}$.
No regularity of $\mu$ is assumed, and one algorithm that depends only on
$B$ attains the rate for every $\mu$ at once. Confidence radii follow the same
law with $B$ replaced by $B/\log(1/\delta)$. An estimator that is unbiased
for every sign law, with uniformly bounded variance and expected work,
exists exactly when $I_\mu=\E_\mu D^{-1}<\infty$; the best variance--work
product then lies between $I_\mu^2/16$ and $713I_\mu^2$. For threshold
probabilities in nested simulation this gives a lower bound of order
$\varepsilon^{-2}\log^2(1/\varepsilon)$, which matches the adaptive
multilevel upper rate on a class satisfying its assumptions.

The answer changes when positive coins are known to be rare,
$\theta\le\rho$. A variational modulus $\Psi_{\mu,\rho}$ bounds the risk
from below and gives the rate for power-law gap distributions with exponent
below or above two. At exponent two it falls short: for $F(d)=(4d)^2$ the
rate is $\min\{\rho,\sqrt{\rho L(1+\log L)/B}\}$, with $L=1+\log(1/\rho)$,
whereas $\Psi$ gives only $\min\{\rho,\sqrt{\rho L/B}\}$. For every known
gap law, rarity bound and budget, the risk is within universal constants of
an entropy--tail selection functional: a selected part of the law pays the
entropy of its inverse-square costs across dyadic scales, and the rest pays
a truncated inverse-gap cost. Selecting everything except the mass $\rho$
of smallest gaps gives a closed formula within a factor
$c_*\approx1.4626$ of the best selection, and no smaller universal factor
is possible. Further results cover deterministic observation caps, fixed
and time-uniform confidence intervals, a stopping-program dual that
certifies the minimax risk within a factor of sixteen, and adaptation to an
unknown gap law.
