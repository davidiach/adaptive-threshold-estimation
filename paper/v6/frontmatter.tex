\begin{abstract}
We estimate the fraction $\theta=\Prob(P>1/2)$ of a population of latent
Bernoulli coins at unit cost per observed flip. Algorithms may create,
revisit, and interleave coins under a uniform expected budget. For any law $\mu$ of $D=|P-1/2|$ on $(0,1/2]$, the minimax RMS
error is comparable to $\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2})$, where
$\omega_\mu(h)=\E_\mu\min\{1,h/D\}$. One algorithm depending only on
the budget attains this rate for every $\mu$. Unbiased estimation with
uniformly finite variance and expected work is possible exactly when
$I_\mu=\E_\mu D^{-1}<\infty$; the optimal uniform variance--work
product lies between $I_\mu^2/16$ and $713I_\mu^2$.

With the additional global rarity constraint $\theta\le\rho$, the
variational quantity $\Psi_{\mu,\rho}$ gives a general lower bound and
characterizes the power-margin regimes below and above exponent two, but
fails at the boundary. For the fixed quadratic law $F(d)=(4d)^2$, put
$L=1+\log(1/\rho)$. The sharp RMS order is
$\min\{\rho,\sqrt{\rho L(1+\log L)/B}\}$,
whereas $\Psi$ gives only $\min\{\rho,\sqrt{\rho L/B}\}$.
For every known magnitude law, rarity bound and positive budget, the
risk is comparable to an entropy--tail selection functional: a selected
part of the law pays its dyadic inverse-square cost entropy, and the
remainder pays a truncated inverse-gap cost. Removing the smallest-gap
mass $\rho$ gives a closed formula whose sharp universal comparison
factor is approximately $1.46255455128$.
For finite laws an exact optimizer selects an upper-gap suffix in each
dyadic band, and a direct proof gives explicit risk bounds. A robust stopping program has
an exact four-prior dual and certifies minimax risk within a factor of
sixteen at a local cap of $\lceil4B/3\rceil$.
