\section{The fresh-outer-state upper-bound transfer}
\label{app:observed-upper}

This appendix completes the observed-state upper bounds used in
\cref{prop:outer,prop:continuous}. The policy samples independent
uniform outer states and assigns one to each original coin label.
It uses the original estimator's observation history and private
randomness, and its output discards the auxiliary outer sequence.

\begin{theorem}[Verified observed-state upper bounds]
\label{thm:observed-upper-verified}
There are observed-state estimator families, selected before the gap
law and deterministic sign-cell model, that use at most $B$ expected
inner observations and attain
\[
 \operatorname{RMS}\le512\,\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \Pr\!\left(
 |\widehat\theta-\theta|>
 8192\,\omega_\mu\!\left(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/B}\right)
 \right)\le\delta
\]
for $B\ge1$ and $0<\delta\le1/4$.
Here $\mu$ is the actual gap law. The statements allow arbitrary
finite measurable sign-cell designs and every physical experiment
with the ordinary fresh-Bernoulli observation rule.

For $\mu=\operatorname{Unif}(0,1/4]$ and
$0<\varepsilon\le e^{-16}$, each guarantee is attained with
\[
 B_\varepsilon
 =131072^2\,\varepsilon^{-2}\log^2(1/\varepsilon)\log16,
\]
using failure probability $1/16$ for the confidence guarantee.
Together with \cref{app:continuous-observed}, this proves both
two-sided complexity rates in the continuous-magnitude observed-state
class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $D(u)$ be the gap map and $s(u)$ the cell sign. The actual
Bernoulli parameter is
$p(u)=1/2+(2s(u)-1)D(u)$.
For a measurable gap set $E$, define the positive-gap submeasure
\[
 \nu_+(E)=\Pr(D(U)\in E,\ s(U)=1).
\]
It satisfies $\nu_+\le\mu$.
Its Radon--Nikodym derivative has a measurable version
$q(d)\in[0,1]$ everywhere, and the checked identity is
$\nu_+=q\,\mu$.
Testing against bounded measurable functions gives
\[
 p(U)\ \sim\
 \int\left[q(d)\,\delta_{1/2+d}
       +(1-q(d))\,\delta_{1/2-d}\right]\mu({\rm d}d),
 \qquad
 \theta=\int q\,{\rm d}\mu.
\]
Thus every deterministic observed-state model has the parameter law
and target required by the original population estimator.

Take an original adaptive estimator with private seed $R$.
Augment its seed by an independent sequence
$U_0,U_1,\ldots$ of uniform outer states.
When its policy requests coin $i$, request one fresh observation at
$U_i$. A repeated label reuses the same state. The selected-state
policy is measurable, depends on no gap law or sign array, and has
the original output as a function of its seed and observation tape.

Conditional on the complete outer sequence, the signs are
deterministic and each requested parameter is $p(U_i)$.
The formal proof first reduces the original sign-mixture transcript
weights at priors zero and one to these fixed-parameter weights.
It then identifies the finite-prefix expectation of the
selected-state policy with the original countable-label creation law.
A finite-kernel conversion retains the full measurable seed and
passes from nonnegative tests to bounded real tests.

The existing population-simulation theorem identifies the complete
observable transcript measure after the auxiliary outer sequence is
discarded. The parameter-law identity above makes this exactly the
original experiment with gap law $\mu$ and conditional sign law $q$.
Consequently the two experiments have equal expected actual work,
squared risk, RMS risk, and failure probabilities. These equalities
include unbounded stopping and preserve infinite risk.

Apply the checked original RMS and confidence estimator families.
The resulting selected-state families are chosen before every
$\mu$, gap map and sign-cell model, which proves the general bounds.

For the uniform law, use
$\omega_\mu(h)=4h[1+\log(1/(4h))]$ for $0<h<1/4$.
The checked budget calculation gives
\[
 8192\,\omega_\mu\!\left(\sqrt{\log16/B_\varepsilon}\right)
 \le\varepsilon,\qquad
 512\,\omega_\mu(B_\varepsilon^{-1/2})\le\varepsilon.
\]
The first inequality gives fixed confidence and the second gives RMS.
\end{proof}
