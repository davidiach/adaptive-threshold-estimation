\section{Rare-event confidence at the effective budget}
\label{app:known-confidence}

Fix a known positive-gap law $\mu$. Write $r_{\rm exp}$ and $r_{\rm hard}$
for the smallest uniform confidence radii in the sense of infima. The first
budget bounds expected work under each admissible sign law. The second
caps the number of purchased flips on every valid observation path.
Put $\ell=\log(1/\delta)$ and $\beta=B/\ell$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:confidence-stopping-dual}
For $B>0$, $0<\delta\le1/4$, and $0<\rho\le1/2$,
$r_{\rm exp}(B,\delta)\ge P_\infty(\beta)/30$.
For $0<\rho\le1/4$ the entropy--tail functional consequently satisfies
\[
 10^{-17}\mathcal K(\beta)\le r_{\rm exp}(B,\delta)
 \le r_{\rm hard}(B,\delta)\le 750000\,\mathcal K(\beta).
\]
For the critical quadratic law let $L=1+\log(1/\rho)$. Both radii are
within universal factors of $\min\{\rho,\sqrt{\rho L(1+\log L)/\beta}\}$.
The upper coefficient is $2670000$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Consider a quartet in the stopping-program dual of Appendix~\ref{sec:genie},
with target separation $\Delta$ and information/work ratio $J$. Its
contribution to $P_\infty(\beta)^2$ is $\Delta^2/(4+\beta J)$.
It suffices to bound positive contributions with finite $J$.
Mix each endpoint equally with the two reference priors, giving each of
the three components weight $1/3$. The separated means now differ by
$\Delta/3$, and the midpoint ratio of the new pair is at most $J$.
Contract the endpoints toward their midpoint by the factor
$a=(1+6\beta J)^{-1/2}$. The ratio becomes at most $a^2J$, the gap
becomes $a\Delta/3$, and the endpoint means still meet the rarity bound.

Write $q,r$ for the contracted priors, $m=(q+r)/2$, $s=(3q+r)/4$ and
$t=(q+3r)/4$. For every capped single-coin rule, the inequality
$(\sqrt x-\sqrt y)^2\le(x-y)^2/(x+y)$ gives squared Hellinger
distance at most $a^2J W_m/4$ in the normalization $1-\int\sqrt{dP\,dQ}$.
Single-coin work is affine in its prior, so $W_m\le2W_s$. Quarter
smoothing bounds the KL divergence between the $s,t$ transcripts by
$8/3$ times the Hellinger distance squared between the $q,r$ transcripts.
Isolate each coin in the finite population tree by simulating the other
coins under $s$. Its work charge is then the expected number of flips
of that coin under $s$. The one-coin KL contributions add under the same
reference. Finite-prefix reconstruction with finite endpoint work gives
the full observable KL bound $(4/3)a^2JB\le2\ell/9$.

The final mean gap is $a\Delta/6$. A radius $\varepsilon<a\Delta/12$
would distinguish these means at error at most $\delta$, requiring KL at
least $\ell/4$. Thus $a\Delta/12\le\varepsilon$. The inequality
$144(1+6x)\le900(4+x)$ for $x\ge0$ bounds the quartet contribution by
$(30\varepsilon)^2$. Taking the dual supremum and the radius infimum
proves the \href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/384de1857b55f86cad3d86e6e5801b69d8eb9519/lean/ThresholdHC/ConfidenceProgram/Lower.lean}{stopping-program lower bound}. Section~\ref{sec:entropy-confidence}
proves the direct comparison
$30\cdot10^{-17}Q(\beta)\le P_\infty(\beta)$, giving the stated
entropy--tail lower coefficient.
The critical lower bound follows from the critical two-prior construction.

\clearpage
For the upper bound, start with an observable rule whose expected work is
at most $b=B/(8k)$ and whose root mean squared error is at most $u$,
uniformly over the rare priors. Choose an odd $k=2m+1$ and reserve
$N=\lfloor B/k\rfloor$ flips per copy. Use separate coin labels and
private seeds for the copies. Accept a decision made after exactly $N$
flips and clip it to $[0,\rho]$. A copy still requesting a flip after its
$N$th reservation returns $\rho/2$. The controller may fill unused
reservations and in all cases spends at most $kN\le\lfloor B\rfloor$.
A timeout requires at least $N+1$ flips in the uncapped copy. Its
probability is at most $b/(N+1)\le1/8$, even when $N=0$.
On a completed copy, clipping can only reduce squared error. Markov's
inequality bounds the probability of completion with error exceeding
$\sqrt8\,u$ by $1/8$ (zero RMS gives zero error almost surely).
The union bound gives failure probability at most $1/4$ for each copy.

Let $I_i$ indicate failure of copy $i$. The copies are independent, so
$\mathbb E\prod_i(1+2I_i)\le(3/2)^k$. On median failure at least $m+1$
copies fail, making the product at least $3^{m+1}$. The median therefore
has \href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/fb4989c2bf21828214d74283ced14be44ec0b58d/lean/ThresholdHC/ConfidenceProgram/Amplify.lean}{failure probability at most $(3/4)^m/2$}. Choose $m=\lceil4\ell\rceil$.
Since $\log(3/4)\le-1/4$ and $\ell\ge1$, the failure probability is at
most $\delta$, $b\le\beta$, and $\beta/b=8k/\ell\le88$. The already
proved budget scaling bounds the base rate at $b$ by $\sqrt{88}$ times
its value at $\beta$. Use the general-law RMS witness with coefficient
$28001$, or the critical estimator with coefficient $100000$. Multiplying
each by $\sqrt{8\cdot88}\le26.7$ gives the two asserted upper bounds.
Finally every hard-cap rule satisfies the expected-work constraint.
\end{proof}
