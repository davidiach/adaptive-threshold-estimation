\section{RMS guarantees under a deterministic observation cap}
\label{app:hard-budget}

A run with expected cost $B$ can buy more than $B$ observations on a
particular path. Let $R^{\rm hard}(B)$ be the minimax RMS risk when every
valid observation path must stop within $\lfloor B\rfloor$ paid flips.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hard-budget-fallback}
Suppose $\theta\in[0,M]$. At each budget $b_j=2^j$, let a base rule use
at most $b_j$ flips in expectation and have squared error at most
$C^2 f(b_j)^2$ in expectation. Assume $C\ge1/2$, $f\ge0$ on $[1/2,\infty)$,
$f(1/2)=M$ and $f(b)\le\sqrt{B/b}\,f(B)$ for $B\ge b\ge1/2$. There
is an observable rule with a pathwise cap of $\lfloor B\rfloor$ and
mean squared error at most $32C^2 f(B)^2$ for $B\ge4$.
The rule uses the base rules, $M$ and $B$. Thus one base family fixed
before the gap law gives one transformed family fixed before the law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Reserve $n_j=4b_j$ flips for stage $j$. Start at $M/2$. Complete as
many whole stages as fit. A run that stops by its last reserved flip
replaces the previous estimate by its output clipped to $[0,M]$.
A run that requests another flip at the cap leaves the previous estimate
in place. Below budget four the rule returns $M/2$ without buying a flip.

Pair each stage index with the local coin label so a revisit uses the
same coin and stages have disjoint labels. Draw independent private seeds.
The checked implementation spends each reserved block in full. After a
stage has finished, a decoder ignores the remaining observations in that
block and recovers the original stopped tape. This gives the same retained
output on every table of persistent coin flips. The total reservation
through stage $j$ is $S_j=4(2^{j+1}-1)$.

Let $D_j$ be the mean squared error after stage $j$, with
$D_{-1}=(M/2-\theta)^2$. Markov's inequality bounds the probability
of a timeout by $1/4$. The previous estimate is independent of the
next stage. On success, its clipped squared error is a nonnegative
part of that stage's unconditional loss. Hence
\[
 D_j\le C^2 f(b_j)^2+\tfrac14 D_{j-1}.
\]
Set $b_{-1}=1/2$. The initial squared error is at most $C^2f(b_{-1})^2$.
At the last completed stage $m$, rate scaling gives
\[
 D_m\le C^2(B/b_m) f(B)^2\sum_{\ell=0}^{m+1}2^{-\ell}.
\]
The next stage does not fit, so $B<S_{m+1}<16b_m$. This proves the bound.
\end{proof}

the hard-cap upper coefficient is $3000$ in front of $\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2})$.
For the \href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/720fc2bda6b24b62a8fbd086d586f1714e03b902/lean/ThresholdHC/HardBudget/Critical.lean}{critical quadratic law}
it is $570000$ in front of
$\min\{\rho,\sqrt{\rho L(1+\log L)/B}\}$, where $L=1+\log(1/\rho)$.
For an \href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/720fc2bda6b24b62a8fbd086d586f1714e03b902/lean/ThresholdHC/HardBudget/Rare.lean}{arbitrary known gap law} and $0<\rho\le1/4$ it is $160000$ in
front of $\mathcal K(B)$. The ordinary and critical bounds use the
expected-budget families with coefficients $512$ and $100000$.
For the general law, choose a base rule at each dyadic allowance with
RMS at most $28001\,\mathcal K(b_j)$ using the positive rate and the
expected-budget coefficient $28000$. Every pathwise cap bounds expected
work, so the corresponding expected-budget lower bounds apply.
