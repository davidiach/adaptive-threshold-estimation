\section{Observable time-uniform confidence for a known gap law}
\label{app:anytime}

Fix a known positive-gap law $\mu$, $0<\rho\le1/4$ and
$0<\delta\le1/4$. The \href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/ffc0c5eb436ff31706d6f46fc135d1831b5a4e61/lean/ThresholdHC/Anytime/Full.lean}{time-uniform theorem} gives a single online procedure with nested intervals
$I(B)$ such that, uniformly over all rare sign priors,
\[
 \Pr\{\theta\in I(B)\text{ for every }B\ge0\}\ge1-\delta.
\]
It purchases at most $\lfloor B\rfloor$ flips by the output at budget $B$.
On the simultaneous-coverage event its half-width, for every $B\ge1$,
is at most
\[
 1500002\,\mathcal K\!\left(\frac{B}{\Lambda(B,\delta)}\right),\qquad
 \Lambda(B,\delta)=\log(1/\delta)+
 2\log\!\left(2+\frac{\log(1+B)}{\log2}\right).
\]
The same coverage event protects the interval at any data-dependent stopping
budget. The matching law-dependent lower bound is proved below.
Adaptation to an unknown gap law remains open.

\paragraph{A prescribed sequence of observable stages.}
Stage $j$ reserves $s_j=4\cdot2^j$ flips and receives failure allocation
$\delta_j=\delta/[(j+1)(j+2)]$. The total allocation is at most $\delta$.
The fixed-confidence upper theorem of Appendix~\ref{app:known-confidence}
and a strict margin above its infimum supply a stage rule with cap $s_j$
and radius
\[
 r_j=750001\,\mathcal K\!\left(\frac{s_j}{\log(1/\delta_j)}\right).
\]
The selection functional is positive and finite at these positive budgets,
so the extra unit in the coefficient gives an attaining rule at this radius.
Choose the whole sequence before observing any data, with separate private
seeds and coin-label namespaces.

The controller starts with $[0,\rho]$. On completion of stage $j$, it clips
the stage estimate to $[0,\rho]$ and intersects the current interval with
the ball of radius $r_j$ around that estimate. It accepts a decision after
the stage's last reserved flip. It spends whole blocks, discarding filler
observations after an early internal stop. Thus stage $j$ finishes after
$p_{j+1}=4(2^{j+1}-1)$ purchased flips. For any budget, output the most recent
completed intersection.

\paragraph{One experiment for all budgets.}
Both endpoints are measurable functions of the same private seed and stopped
observation history. Increasing the budget preserves every earlier action
and \href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/ec31ac9931c8464fe89f5a4b043a6b2a23e4a65b/lean/ThresholdHC/Anytime/Compatibility.lean}{observed prefix}. The simulation identity maps the original hidden-gap
population onto the independent stage inputs in this shared experiment.
A pathwise-capped stage has stopped by its final reservation almost surely,
and clipping can only decrease its absolute error. Therefore its completed
center has failure probability at most $\delta_j$. A union bound over all
stages gives simultaneous coverage. Intersecting retains the true parameter
on that event, and choosing a stopping budget after seeing the observations
retains the same guarantee.

\paragraph{Width between stage completions.}
Let $k$ be the number of completed stages at budget $B$. Then
$p_k\le B<p_{k+1}$ and
$k\le\log(1+B)/\log2$. If $k=j+1>0$, these inequalities imply
$B<4s_j$ and $\log(1/\delta_j)\le\Lambda(B,\delta)$.
Monotonicity and the square-root budget scaling of $\mathcal K$ give
\[
 r_j\le750001\,\mathcal K\!\left(\frac{B}{4\Lambda(B,\delta)}\right)
       \le1500002\,\mathcal K\!\left(\frac{B}{\Lambda(B,\delta)}\right).
\]
The running intersection is contained in the last stage's ball. If $k=0$,
then $B<4$, and the selection functional at $B/\Lambda$ equals $\rho$.
The initial interval therefore satisfies the same bound.

\subsection{Matching lower bound for every gap law}

Fix any probability law $\mu$ on positive gaps, $0<\rho\le1/4$ and
$0<\delta\le1/4$. One common online controller reports measurable intervals
after every integer physical budget. Suppose simultaneous coverage and a
nonnegative deterministic half-width envelope $t(n)$ hold jointly with
probability at least $1-\delta$, under every admissible rare sign prior.
Put $\lambda(n)=\log(1/\delta)+\log\log n$.

\begin{theorem}[Law-dependent time-uniform lower bound]
For every integer $M$, there is an integer $n\ge\max(M,3)$ such that
\[
 t(n)\ge10^{-22}\mathcal K_{\mu,\rho}\bigl(n/\lambda(n)\bigr).
 \tag{TU1}
\]
No monotonicity assumption on $t$ is needed.
\end{theorem}

The proof below is formalized for the original observable hidden-gap
experiment. Its prefix contains the private seed and actual observations;
the controller may adapt acquisition labels, revisit coins, or stop early.
The conclusion is an infinitely-often bound on a deterministic envelope,
not a pointwise bound on every random interval or at every budget.

\paragraph{First-crossing testing.}
Let $Q$ have mean $\theta$ and let $P_k$ have mean $\theta+a_k$, where
$a_k>0$ and $3a_k\le a_i$ for $i<k$. Suppose the observed prefix at an
increasing budget $N_k$ has
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_k^{(N_k)}\Vert Q^{(N_k)})\le K_k$.
If $t(N_k)<a_k/6$ at every step, define
\[
 E_k=\{\widehat\theta_{N_k}>\theta+a_k/2\},\qquad
 A_k=E_k\setminus\bigcup_{i<k}E_i.
\]
On simultaneous accuracy under $P_k$, the first crossing occurs exactly
at $k$. Under $Q$, there is no crossing. Hence $P_k(A_k)\ge1-\delta$,
the $A_k$ are disjoint, and $\sum_k Q(A_k)\le\delta$.
Binary data processing and binary entropy at most $\log2$ imply
\[
 Q(A_k)\ge\tfrac14\exp\{-K_k/(1-\delta)\}.
 \tag{TU2}
\]
Thus these exponential terms must be summable. Apply the same reasoning
to every tail: a nonsummable testing schedule forces
$t(N_k)\ge a_k/6$ infinitely often. Independence across prefixes is
unnecessary.

For any positive antitone scale $s_k$, take
\[
 N_k=\left\lfloor\frac{1-\delta}{2}
                  \frac{\log(k+2)}{s_k^2}\right\rfloor.
 \tag{TU3}
\]
This is monotone and unbounded. When $K_k=N_ks_k^2$, the testing terms
dominate $(k+2)^{-1/2}$, so they are nonsummable. Also, eventually,
$N_ks_k^2\ge\log(k+2)/8$ for $\delta\le1/2$.

\paragraph{Finite asymptotic coefficient.}
Let $C=\sup_{B>0}\sqrt B\,\mathcal K(B)$. If the inverse gap is integrable,
then $0<C<\infty$: selection of the empty core gives
$\mathcal K(B)\le B^{-1/2}\int d^{-1}\,\mu(dd)$, while small-budget
saturation gives positivity.

Choose $B$ with $\sqrt B\,\mathcal K(B)>C/2$.
Attained infinite-cap four-prior duality and the existing
$10^{-17}$ stopping-program comparison supply an admissible certificate
with mean gap $\Delta>0$ and information bound $J>0$ satisfying
\[
 (10^{-17}C/2)^2J\le\Delta^2.
 \tag{TU4}
\]
Absorbing its variance and work references into the endpoint priors gives
a central segment with prefix KL at most
$(64/3)Jn(x-y)^2$ and mean difference $(y-x)\Delta/3$.
Use shifts $u_k=4^{-k}/8$ from the central reference and
$s_k=\sqrt{(64/3)J}\,u_k$. The shifts satisfy the required separation.
Equations (TU2)--(TU3) force $t(N_k)\ge u_k\Delta/18$ infinitely often.

Set $h(n)=\sqrt{\lambda(n)/n}$. The geometric schedule gives
$\lambda(N_k)\le3\log(k+2)$ eventually, so $h(N_k)\le5s_k$.
By the definition of $C$,
\[
 \mathcal K(N_k/\lambda(N_k))
 \le C h(N_k)\le25C\sqrt J\,u_k.
 \tag{TU5}
\]
Together with (TU4), this proves (TU1). This branch actually requires only
finiteness of $C$, a weaker premise than inverse-gap integrability.

\paragraph{Infinite inverse moment and capped profiles.}
Choose $0<\tau\le1/4$ with $0<\mu\{d\le\tau\}\le\rho$ and put
\[
 g_h(d)=\mathbf1_{\{d\le\tau\}}\min(1,h/d),\quad
 T(h)=\int g_h\,d\mu,\quad
 \varepsilon_j=\tau4^{-j},\quad W_j=T(\varepsilon_j).
\]
Such a small-gap region exists by dominated convergence. The inverse gap
on it is still nonintegrable, because the complementary inverse gap is
bounded by $1/\tau$. Monotone convergence of $g_h/h$ gives
$T(h)/h\to\infty$ as $h\downarrow0$.
Selecting the empty core therefore proves, for all small positive $h$,
\[
 \mathcal K(h^{-2})\le T(h)+h/\tau\le2T(h).
 \tag{TU6}
\]
Also $T(ah)\le aT(h)$ for $a\ge1$. No quantile comparison factor is needed
for this branch.

The sequence $W_j$ is positive, antitone and tends to zero, with
$W_{j+1}\ge W_j/4$. Starting at $j_0=0$, choose $j_{k+1}$ as the first
later index with $W_{j_{k+1}}\le W_{j_k}/4$.
For $j_k\le j<j_{k+1}$, minimality gives $W_j>W_{j_k}/4$.

Let $q_0=1/4$ on $\{d\le\tau\}$ and zero elsewhere, and define
\[
 q_k=q_0+(g_{\varepsilon_{j_k}}-g_{\varepsilon_{j_{k+1}}})/32.
\]
These are admissible measurable rare sign priors. Their mean increments
$a_k=(W_{j_k}-W_{j_{k+1}})/32$ satisfy
$a_k\ge3W_{j_k}/128$ and $3a_k\le a_i$ for $i<k$.
The gap-weighted contrast is at most $\varepsilon_{j_k}/32$.

\paragraph{Adaptive information.}
On the differing region all priors lie in $[1/4,9/32]$ and gaps are at
most $1/4$. After any fixed history on one coin, its posterior sign
probability is Lipschitz in its prior with coefficient at most $16/3$.
The resulting Bernoulli observation KL is controlled by a constant times
the squared gap-weighted contrast. Outside the region the laws agree,
including deterministic observations.
The finite-transcript chain argument therefore gives
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_k^{(n)}\Vert Q^{(n)})\le n\varepsilon_{j_k}^2.
 \tag{TU7}
\]
Integrating over the private seed and the iid hidden-gap array, then
forgetting the gaps by data processing, proves (TU7) for the actual
observable prefix. Revisits, adaptive labels and early stopping are included.

\paragraph{Dense blocks.}
If $j_k\le(k+2)^2$ eventually, use (TU3) with $s_k=\varepsilon_{j_k}$.
Testing forces $t(N_k)\ge W_{j_k}/256$ infinitely often.
The polynomial index bound gives $\lambda(N_k)\le3\log(k+2)$ eventually,
and hence $h(N_k)\le5\varepsilon_{j_k}$. Equation (TU6) yields
\[
 \mathcal K(N_k/\lambda(N_k))\le10W_{j_k}.
\]
This proves the stronger coefficient $1/2560$.

\paragraph{Sparse blocks.}
Otherwise $j_k>k^2$ infinitely often. If eventually every increment were
smaller than $\sqrt{j_k}$, then
$\sqrt{j_{k+1}}\le\sqrt{j_k}+1/2$, contradicting that growth.
Thus $j_{k+1}-j_k\ge\sqrt{j_k}$ infinitely often.
For a sufficiently large such start $j=j_k$, set
\[
 a=\lfloor\sqrt j/2\rfloor,\qquad m=j+a,\qquad
 n=\lceil16^m/\tau^2\rceil.
\]
Then $m+1<j_{k+1}$, $n\varepsilon_{m+1}^2\le1/8$, and these budgets
tend to infinity. Elementary geometric bounds give
$\lambda(n)\le3(j+1)\le16^a\le n\varepsilon_j^2$.
Therefore $h(n)\le\varepsilon_j$ and (TU6) bounds
$\mathcal K(n/\lambda(n))$ by $2W_j$.

Use the single alternative $q=q_0+g_{\varepsilon_{m+1}}/32$.
Its mean gap is $W_{m+1}/32$ and its prefix KL is at most $1/8$.
If $t(n)$ were smaller than half the gap, simultaneous accuracy would give
a test of power at least $3/4$ and size at most $1/4$.
Binary Pinsker would force KL at least $1/2$, a contradiction.
Consequently
\[
 t(n)\ge W_{m+1}/64>W_j/256
       \ge\mathcal K(n/\lambda(n))/512.
 \tag{TU8}
\]
Both infinite-moment cases imply (TU1).

\paragraph{Intervals and formal scope.}
On joint coverage and half-width control, the midpoint of the interval
has error at most $t(n)$ at every budget. The endpoints' measurability
makes the midpoint observable, completing the interval theorem.

The \href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/a6c77f546604cb6df7df67802e0061d1345fa2ca/lean/ThresholdHC/AnytimeLower/Intervals.lean}{complete interval lower bound}
and its \href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/a6c77f546604cb6df7df67802e0061d1345fa2ca/lean/ThresholdHC/AnytimeLower/Full.lean}{online-estimate counterpart}
are Lean-checked. The final audit independently restates fourteen
asymptotic and interval statements and checks their transitive axioms.
The separate sequential and tail-family audits contain eight and fifteen
statements respectively. This is the deterministic-envelope asymptotic
comparison; adaptation to the oracle rate for an unknown gap law remains open.
