\subsection{Summary of results}\label{sec:summary}
\Cref{tab:results} collects the main statements. Throughout, $\mu$ is the
law of a coin's gap $D=|P-1/2|$, the budget $B$ bounds the expected number
of flips, and $\asymp$ hides universal positive constants unless a
subscript names a dependence. The first block of the table takes the worst
case over every sign law at a fixed gap law, in the model of
\cref{sec:model}. The second block adds the constraint $\theta\le\rho$,
with $\mu$, $\rho$ and $B$ known to the algorithm, in the model of
\cref{sec:rare-model}.

\multicolumn{3}{@{}l}{\emph{Every sign law: any gap law $\mu$ on $(0,1/2]$,
 $B\ge1$, $0<\delta\le1/4$}}\\[3pt]
Minimax RMS error
 & $\tfrac1{64}\,\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2})\le R_\mu(B)\le C\,\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2})$,
   where $\omega_\mu(h)=\E_\mu\min\{1,h/D\}$. One algorithm that depends
   only on $B$ attains the upper bound for every $\mu$.
 & \cref{thm:main}; proof in \cref{sec:lower,sec:classifier,sec:minimax-proof}\\[3pt]
Confidence radius
 & $r_\mu(B,\delta)\asymp\omega_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/B}\bigr)$, again
   with one algorithm for every $\mu$.
 & \cref{thm:main}\\[3pt]
Unbiased estimation
 & An estimator that is unbiased for every sign law, with uniformly
   finite variance $V$ and expected work $W$, exists if and only if
   $I_\mu=\E_\mu D^{-1}<\infty$. Then
   $I_\mu^2/16\le\inf VW\le713\,I_\mu^2$, and one estimator attains the
   upper bound for every such $\mu$.
 & \cref{thm:unbiased}; proof in \cref{sec:unbiased-proof,sec:713}\\[3pt]
Unknown gap law
 & The three statements above hold uniformly over every population whose
   gaps are stochastically no smaller than those of $\mu$.
 & \cref{cor:envelope}\\[3pt]
Margin conditions
 & If $\Prob(|P-1/2|\le t)\le Ct^\alpha$, the work needed for accuracy
   $\varepsilon$ at confidence $1-\delta$ is of order
   $\log(1/\delta)$ times $\varepsilon^{-2/\alpha}$ for $\alpha<1$,
   $\varepsilon^{-2}\log^2(1/\varepsilon)$ for $\alpha=1$ and
   $\varepsilon^{-2}$ for $\alpha>1$, for small $\varepsilon$. Logarithmic
   corrections to a linear margin interpolate between the last two cases.
 & \cref{cor:power,cor:log}; \cref{tab:rates}\\[3pt]
Smooth densities
 & The rate $\varepsilon^{-2}\log^2(1/\varepsilon)\log(1/\delta)$ persists
   when $P$ has a density bounded in $C^s$, for each fixed $s$.
 & \cref{thm:smooth}; \cref{app:smooth-density}\\[3pt]
Observed outer states
 & With observable states and no regularity across states, the cost at
   RMS error $\varepsilon$ is of order
   $\varepsilon^{-2}\log^2(1/\varepsilon)$ on a class satisfying the
   assumptions of~\cite{gileshajiali}.
 & \cref{cor:nested}, \cref{prop:continuous}; \cref{app:finite-observed,app:continuous-observed,app:observed-upper}\\[3pt]
\addlinespace[4pt]
\multicolumn{3}{@{}l}{\emph{Rare events: $\theta\le\rho$ with
 $0<\rho\le1/4$, and $\mu$, $\rho$, $B$ known}}\\[3pt]
Variational lower bound
 & $R_{\mu,\rho}(B)\ge c\,\Psi_{\mu,\rho}(B^{-1/2})$ for $\Psi$
   in~\eqref{eq:v5-psi}, whose optimizer and value are explicit.
 & \cref{sec:rare}; \cref{loc:thm:PsiLower}\\[3pt]
Power-law gaps
 & For $F(d)=(d/a)^\alpha$, $B\ge1$ and $x=(a\sqrt B)^{-1}$, the RMS error is of
   order $\min\{\rho,x^\alpha\}$ for $\alpha<1$,
   $\min\{\rho,x[1+\log_+(\rho/x)]\}$ for $\alpha=1$,
   $\min\{\rho,\rho^{1-1/\alpha}x\}$ for $1<\alpha<2$ and
   $\min\{\rho,\sqrt\rho\,x\}$ for $\alpha>2$, with constants depending
   on $\alpha$.
 & \cref{sec:rare}; \cref{app:localization,app:above-two,app:sublinear-power,app:shifted-power}\\[3pt]
Critical quadratic law
 & For $F(d)=(4d)^2$ and $B\ge1$,
   $R_{\mu,\rho}(B)\asymp\min\{\rho,\sqrt{\rho L(1+\log L)/B}\}$, whereas
   $\Psi_{\mu,\rho}(B^{-1/2})\asymp\min\{\rho,\sqrt{\rho L/B}\}$.
 & \cref{thm:v5-critical}; \cref{app:critical}\\[3pt]
Entropy--tail law
 & $R_{\mu,\rho}(B)\asymp\mathcal K_{\mu,\rho}(B)$ for every $\mu$ and
   every $B>0$, where $\mathcal K$ is the selection
   functional~\eqref{eq:scalar-selection}.
 & \cref{thm:entropy-tail-law}; \crefrange{sec:entropy-tail-upper}{sec:transfer-foundations}\\[3pt]
Closed quantile form
 & $\mathcal K\le\Q\le c_*\mathcal K$ with
   $\Q(B)=\min\{\rho,\,T_h+h\sqrt{\rho\Gamma_\kappa}\}$
   from~\eqref{fg:eq:closed-functional}, so $R_{\mu,\rho}\asymp\Q$. The
   factor $c_*\approx1.4626$ cannot be lowered.
 & \cref{fg:thm:direct-main,fg:cor:general-laws,thm:sharp-quantile-factor}; \cref{app:finite-direct}\\[3pt]
$\Psi$ in closed form
 & $\Q^0/\sqrt2\le\Psi_{\mu,\rho}(h)\le\Q^0$, where $\Q^0$ is $\Q$ with the
   cost entropy $\Gamma_\kappa$ replaced by the inverse-square cost
   $A_\kappa$. Hence $\Psi\le\mathcal K$, and
   $R_{\mu,\rho}\le C\sqrt{1+E_\kappa}\,\Psi$ with $E_\kappa$ the entropy
   of the band costs of the quantile core.
 & \cref{prop:psi-closed,thm:psi-gap}\\[3pt]
Through the critical exponent
 & For $F(d)=(d/a)^\alpha$ with $3/2\le\alpha\le3$ and $x=(a\sqrt B)^{-1}$,
   $R_{\mu,\rho}(B)\asymp\min\bigl\{\rho,\
   x\sqrt{\rho\,\Phi_\alpha(\rho)[1+\log N_\alpha(\rho)]}\bigr\}$ with
   constants independent of $\alpha$, for $\Phi_\alpha$ and $N_\alpha$
   in~\eqref{eq:power-phi-n}. A formula of the same kind holds for
   $0<\alpha\le4$ and for confidence radii.
 & \cref{thm:power-uniform}, \cref{cor:critical-window}\\[3pt]
Stopping programs
 & The cap-$N$ program $P_N$ has an exact four-prior dual, and
   $R_{\mu,\rho}\le P_N\le16R_{\mu,\rho}$ at $N=\lceil4B/3\rceil$.
 & \cref{sec:certificates}; \crefrange{sec:flow-foundations}{sec:computation}, \cref{thm:uniform-cap-rate}\\[3pt]
Deterministic caps
 & The rates for $R_\mu$, for the critical law and for
   $\mathcal K_{\mu,\rho}$ remain valid when every run must stop within
   $\lfloor B\rfloor$ flips.
 & \cref{app:hard-budget}\\[3pt]
Confidence
 & For $0<\delta\le1/4$ the confidence radius is of order
   $\mathcal K_{\mu,\rho}\bigl(B/\log(1/\delta)\bigr)$ under an expected
   budget and under a deterministic cap.
 & \cref{thm:confidence-stopping-dual,thm:entropy-confidence-explicit}\\[3pt]
Time-uniform intervals
 & One procedure gives nested intervals that cover $\theta$ at every
   budget at once, with half-width at most a constant times
   $\mathcal K_{\mu,\rho}\bigl(B/\Lambda(B,\delta)\bigr)$, where
   $\Lambda$ adds an iterated logarithm of $B$ to $\log(1/\delta)$. A
   lower bound of the same form holds at infinitely many budgets.
 & \cref{app:anytime}\\[3pt]
Unknown gap law
 & The same time-uniform rate is attained without knowing $\mu$: after an
   explicit pilot cost when $\mu$ lies in a supplied finite family, and
   after an onset depending on $\mu$ in general.
 & \cref{thm:finite-family-adaptation,thm:arbitrary-law-adaptation}\\

Three limits apply to every row. The results are minimax over sign laws at
a fixed gap law; they are not instance-optimal for an individual
population. Budgets bound expected work unless a row says otherwise. The
universal constants are explicit but conservative, and the rare-event
constants in particular are far from practical: the entropy--tail
comparison has lower coefficient $10^{-17}$ and upper coefficient $33000$
in~\eqref{eq:explicit-main-constants}.

\subsection{How the paper is organized}\label{sec:organization}
The paper has two parts, a verification section and appendices.

\emph{Every sign law (\crefrange{sec:model}{sec:outer}).}
\Cref{sec:model} states the model and the two main theorems.
\Cref{sec:lower} proves the information bound behind every lower bound of
this part, and \cref{sec:classifier} builds the capped classifier and the
randomized multilevel estimator behind every upper bound.
\Cref{sec:minimax-proof,sec:unbiased-proof} combine them into the minimax
law and the unbiased characterization, and \cref{sec:713} sharpens the
variance--work constant to $713$. \Cref{sec:rates,sec:smooth,sec:outer}
give consequences for margin conditions, smooth densities and observed
outer states.

\emph{Rare events (\crefrange{sec:rare}{sec:discussion}).}
\Cref{sec:rare} defines the rare-event experiment and states the
variational lower bound, the power-margin rates and the critical quadratic
law; \cref{sec:all-sizes} extends the sign-entropy input of the critical
lower bound to every number of gaps. \Cref{sec:entropy-tail} states the
entropy--tail law, \cref{fg:sec:model} the closed quantile form and
\cref{sec:certificates} the stopping programs.
\Cref{sec:related-work,sec:discussion} discuss related work, consequences
and limits.

\emph{Verification (\cref{sec:verification}).} This section gives, for
each claim, the formal statement that was checked and its scope, then the
provenance of the work and the open questions.

\emph{Appendices.} \Cref{app:localization,app:above-two,app:critical}
prove the results of \cref{sec:rare}.
\Cref{sec:entropy-tail-upper,sec:matching-lower} prove the two halves of
the entropy--tail law, using the single-unit and transfer tools of
\cref{sec:uf-foundations,sec:lower-foundations,sec:transfer-foundations}.
\Crefrange{sec:flow-foundations}{sec:computation} develop the stopping
programs. \Cref{app:finite-direct} proves the closed quantile form
directly for finite laws, and \cref{app:optimal-quantile-factor}
identifies $c_*$. \Cref{app:uniform-cap-rate,app:hard-budget,app:known-confidence}
treat observation caps and fixed confidence;
\cref{app:sublinear-power,app:shifted-power} give direct proofs of the
power-margin rates below exponent two; \cref{app:anytime} treats
time-uniform confidence and unknown gap laws; and
\crefrange{app:smooth-density}{app:observed-upper} carry the results of
\cref{sec:smooth,sec:outer} to the adaptive experiment.

\emph{Reading paths.} \Crefrange{sec:model}{sec:minimax-proof} are
self-contained and give the main law with its proof. The rare-event part
can be read from \cref{sec:rare} after the model of \cref{sec:model}; it
cites the first part wherever it reuses a construction. Its statements
are in \cref{sec:rare,sec:entropy-tail,fg:sec:model} and its proofs are in
the appendices. \Cref{app:known-confidence,app:anytime} build on the
stopping programs.

\subsection{Notation}\label{sec:notation-table}
\Cref{tab:notation} lists the symbols used throughout. Several letters
change meaning between parts of the paper, because the parts were written
separately. The sign of a coin is $S\in\{0,1\}$, with observation mean
$1/2+(2S-1)D$, except in \cref{fg:sec:model} and \cref{app:finite-direct},
where it is $S\in\{-1,+1\}$ with mean $1/2+SD$. The letter $h$ is a free
gap scale up to \cref{sec:rare} and is fixed as the budget scale
$B^{-1/2}$ from \cref{sec:entropy-tail} on. Squared Hellinger distance is
written $H^2$ in \cref{sec:unbiased-proof}, $h(P,Q)$ in \cref{app:critical}
and $\mathfrak h$ in \cref{fg:sec:model} and
\cref{sec:lower-foundations,sec:transfer-foundations,app:finite-direct}.
The letter $H$ has two other uses: $H=1+\log L$ in
\cref{sec:rare,sec:all-sizes} and \cref{app:critical}, and
$H_\tau=\int d^{-1}\,\tau(dd)$ in \cref{sec:entropy-tail,fg:sec:model}.
The script letter $\mathcal G$ is the class of populations
$\mathcal G(\mu)$ in \crefrange{sec:model}{sec:outer} and the cost entropy
$\mathcal G(a)$ from \cref{sec:entropy-tail} on, while $G$ is a population
law and $G_N$ a program value in \cref{sec:certificates}. Likewise
$\mathcal Q_\rho$ is a class of sign laws and $\Q(B)$ the closed quantile
functional, which some passages write $Q(B)$. Finally $a=1/2$ is the largest gap
in \cref{sec:model}, while $a$ is the scale of a power law in
\cref{sec:rates,sec:rare}.
