\subsection{Oracle-rate adaptation within a supplied finite family}
\label{sec:finite-family-adaptation}

A supplied finite family permits eventual adaptation, even when its
supports and gap locations differ. The identification cost is explicit.

Let $\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_r$ be distinct probability laws on $(0,1/2]$, with
$r\ge2$, and fix known $0<\rho\le1/4$, $0<\delta\le1/4$. The algorithm knows
the family but not its true member. Under that member the sign can depend
arbitrarily on the gap, with total positive mass at most $\rho$.
Write
\[
 a_{i,k}=\int(2d)^{2k}\,\mu_i(dd),\qquad k\ge1.
\]
Choose $m\ge1$ for which the first $m$ moment signatures are distinct, and put
\[
 \eta=\min_{i\ne j}\max_{1\le k\le m}|a_{i,k}-a_{j,k}|,\qquad
 n=\left\lceil\frac{32}{\eta^2}\log\frac{4m}{\delta}\right\rceil,\qquad
 H=2mn.
 \tag{FA1}
\]
Such an $m$ exists and $\eta>0$, as proved below. These quantities are
determined before any observations are purchased.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-family adaptation]\label{thm:finite-family-adaptation}
There is one observable online procedure with nested intervals $I(B)$ and
a physical cap of $B$ flips at every integer budget $B\ge0$. Under each
candidate $\mu_i$ and every admissible sign law, with probability at least
$1-\delta$ it simultaneously covers the positive fraction at every budget
and, for every $B\ge2H$, satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{halfwidth} I(B)\le
 3000004\,\mathcal K_{\mu_i,\rho}
       \!\left(\frac{B}{\Lambda(B,\delta)}\right).
 \tag{FA2}
\]
Here $\Lambda$ is the known-law time-uniform inflation defined at the start
of this appendix. A singleton family uses the known-law procedure directly.
\end{theorem}

The additive cost $H$ depends on the family; no oracle comparison is
claimed before it has been paid.

\paragraph{Sign-neutral observations.}
For a fresh coin buy $2k$ flips $X_1,\ldots,X_{2k}$ and record the observable
statistic
\[
 Z_k=\prod_{\ell=1}^{2k}(2X_\ell-1)\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
Conditionally on its persistent gap $d$ and sign $s$, the flips are iid and
\[
 \mathbb E[Z_k\mid d,s]=(2d)^{2k}.
 \tag{FA3}
\]
Indeed each factor has conditional mean $2d$ or $-2d$, and the exponent is
even. Thus $\mathbb E_{\mu_i}Z_k=a_{i,k}$, irrespective of how the sign
depends on the gap. Fresh coins supply independent parity observations.
The pilot observes only identities and paid flips.

\paragraph{Separation by finitely many moments.}
Push a candidate law forward by the injective map $d\mapsto(2d)^2$ into
$[0,1]$. Equality of all positive moments, together with total mass one,
would imply equality of integrals of all polynomials. Uniform polynomial
approximation then gives equality of integrals of all continuous functions
on this compact interval, hence equality of the probability measures.
The inverse map on $(0,1]$ is measurable, so the original laws would be
equal as well. Each distinct pair therefore has a differing positive
moment. There are finitely many pairs; take $m$ at least as large as all
of their witnessing indices. This proves (FA1) is well-defined.

For finite-support candidates with $s$ points in their combined support,
moments $0,\ldots,s-1$ form an invertible Vandermonde system on the distinct
numbers $(2d)^2$. Total mass is already one, so $m\le s-1$ suffices.

\paragraph{A paid identification pilot.}
Take $n$ fresh coins, each with $2m$ paid flips. Record all even-prefix
parities on each coin; let $\widehat a_k$ average its $k$th parity across
coins. All $H=2mn$ flips are charged, including revisits. Independence
across coins suffices; coordinates need not be independent.
Hoeffding's inequality for variables in $[-1,1]$ gives
\[
 \Pr_{\mu_i}\{|\widehat a_k-a_{i,k}|>\eta/4\}
 \le2\exp(-n\eta^2/32).
\]
The union bound over the $m$ coordinates is at most $\delta/2$ by (FA1).
Choose the first candidate whose every coordinate lies within $\eta/4$
of the empirical signature, and use the first candidate if none is
compatible. When every coordinate is accurate, the true candidate is
compatible. Two compatible candidates would have distance at most
$\eta/2<\eta$, contradicting separation. Thus the pilot selects the true
law with probability at least $1-\delta/2$, uniformly over its sign laws.
The finite threshold rule is measurable.

\paragraph{One controller and one simultaneous event.}
During the pilot output $[0,\rho]$, pausing between flips as needed.
After $H$ flips run the selected candidate's known-law controller with
failure allowance $\delta/2$, fresh labels and independent private
randomness. Its remaining budget is $B-H$. Actions have consistent
prefixes across budgets. The known-law cap is pathwise, so the physical
cap holds even when identification fails.

Under true candidate $i$, its hypothetical controller on the fresh stream
has simultaneous failure probability at most $\delta/2$. Correct
identification makes it the actual controller. Actual failure is therefore
contained in the union of pilot error and this one controller's failure.
No union over candidates or independence of these events is needed.
Fresh labels and randomness give the required post-pilot marginal law.
The two allowances sum to $\delta$. Nested intersections preserve
simultaneous coverage, including at data-dependent stopping budgets.

\paragraph{Oracle width after the pilot.}
On that joint event, for $B-H\ge1$ the known-law theorem bounds the
half-width by
\[
 1500002\,\mathcal K_{\mu_i,\rho}
 \!\left(\frac{B-H}{\Lambda(B-H,\delta/2)}\right).
\]
For $B\ge2H$, the remaining budget is at least $B/2$. Moreover
\[
 \Lambda(B-H,\delta/2)\le\Lambda(B,\delta)+\log2
                    \le2\Lambda(B,\delta).
\]
The last inequality uses $\delta\le1/4$.
Thus the effective remaining budget is at least
$B/[4\Lambda(B,\delta)]$. Monotonicity and square-root budget scaling of
$\mathcal K$ prove (FA2). The family with one member needs no pilot.
