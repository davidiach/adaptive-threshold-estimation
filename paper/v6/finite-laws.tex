\section{The closed quantile form}\label{fg:sec:model}
The selection in \eqref{eq:scalar-selection} ranges over submeasures of
$\mu$. Remove exactly the smallest-gap mass $\rho$, split a boundary atom
if needed, and compute the entropy cost of the remaining core. Add the
truncated inverse-gap cost of the removed mass, then cap the sum at $\rho$
to obtain the closed value $Q$.
This value is within a constant factor of the selection functional.
\Cref{fg:prop:quantile-selection} proves
$\mathcal K\le\Q\le(1+2\sqrt2)\mathcal K$ by a short argument, the
paragraph after \cref{fg:cor:general-laws} states the sharper factor
$8/5$, and \cref{thm:sharp-quantile-factor} identifies the least possible
factor $c_*\approx1.4626$ as the root of a scalar equation.
For finite laws, Theorem~\ref{fg:thm:direct-main} proves the
resulting closed formula with explicit constants, by an argument that does
not invoke Theorem~\ref{thm:entropy-tail-law}.
Corollary~\ref{fg:cor:general-laws} extends the formula to every law
through that theorem.

The direct lower proof in \cref{fg:sec:matching-lower} first obtains
$R(B)\ge T_h/32$ by a posterior-martingale calculation and an adaptive
midpoint decomposition. It then filters the dyadic core costs. Either
the tail already controls the desired rate, or the filter preserves a
constant fraction of the core entropy. Grouping and trimming the
surviving bands produces conditional experiments that satisfy the
required rarity and saturation constraints; their lower bounds add
through a common-pair argument. The upper bound in
\cref{fg:sec:direct-upper} applies the construction of
\cref{sec:entropy-tail-upper} to the quantile split as a known design
choice. \Cref{fg:sec:assembly-contract} assembles all branches and
tracks the constants. Both directions use the single-unit and transfer
results of
\cref{sec:uf-foundations,sec:lower-foundations,sec:transfer-foundations};
no statistical selection theorem is assumed.

\paragraph{Sharper comparison and exact finite selection.}
For every law on $(0,1/2]$, $0<\rho\le1/4$ and $B>0$, the selection
functional satisfies $\mathcal K\le Q\le(8/5)\mathcal K$. The quantile
tail gives the floor $T_h\le\mathcal K$, and $Q=\mathcal K=\rho$ when
$h=B^{-1/2}$ reaches or exceeds the quantile threshold.

For a law with finite support, a hard selection attains the minimum over
all submeasures before the cap at $\rho$. Within each canonical dyadic
band it selects a suffix in increasing gap order. Moving selected
inverse-square cost between two atoms in the same band preserves the
band total and hence the entropy. At gap $d$ the tail cost saved per unit is
$d^2\min\{1,h/d\}=\min\{d^2,hd\}$, strictly increasing in $d$.
If a selected atom had a larger unselected neighbor in its band, moving
a sufficiently small positive amount of cost to that neighbor would
improve the objective. Thus if occupied band $j$ has $n_j$ atoms,
searching at most $\prod_j(n_j+1)$ choices suffices. The cut can differ
from band to band. Section~\ref{sec:verification} links both proofs.

For repeated suffix costs, choices with the same multiset have identical
entropy. Keeping only the smallest ordinary remainder in each class reduces
the search to at most $\binom{m+r}{r}$ terminal states for $m$ occupied
bands and $r$ distinct positive suffix costs.
The \href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/7332a2c199fffd977d405f30b92c8740b58c5179/docs/BAND_COST_SELECTION.md}{selection algorithm}
certifies an optimizer on a 64-band example using 65 terminal states.
Section~\ref{sec:verification} gives the formal menu-algorithm scope and
the finite-grid error bound.
