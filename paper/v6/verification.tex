\paragraph{Realizing the distribution-blind upper algorithm.}
The formal construction follows the two-pilot calibration strategy in
\cref{sec:minimax-proof}. A stopped cutoff call with cap $N=2^\ell$ is
padded by one unused flip when its original work is zero. Its output
is unchanged, and every call advances the observed history. Two independent
first-success pilots select the number of fresh production calls. The
calibrated quantities have expected work at most $4N$ and mean-square
error at most $12751\omega_\mu(N^{-1/2})^2$.

These estimates are transferred to a measurable controller in the original
experiment by exact seed and population path identities. Positive mean
marked cost proves almost-sure pilot termination. For $B\ge4$, choosing
$4N\le B<8N$ and comparing moduli yields coefficient $512$; for $1\le B<4$
a zero-work estimator on the same seed space suffices. The coefficient
includes padding and conservative rounding and is not claimed optimal.
The full audit checks the unchanged specification and all transitive axioms;
the earlier component records retain their historical incomplete status.

\paragraph{Realizing the confidence upper algorithm.}
Put $m=\lceil2\log(1/\delta)\rceil$ and $k=2m+1$. Each of $k$ independent
calibrated batches receives expected budget $B/k$. Its failure probability
at radius $512\omega_\mu(N^{-1/2})$ is at most $1/8$. A union bound over
majority subsets puts the median's failure probability below $2^{-m}\le\delta$.
The controller uses disjoint pair-numbered coin labels and independent
copies of the seed. Exact finite execution identities prove that its
output is the median and that actual work is the sum of the batch costs.
The product law and almost-sure termination are derived in the original
experiment. Since $k\le7\log(1/\delta)$, budget rounding and modulus
comparison give the coefficient $8192$. A zero-work rule handles the
small-budget regime. The radius is a theoretical function of $\mu$, not
a confidence interval computed from the observations alone.

The lower proof applies a binary confidence test to the same adaptive
transcript information bound. It proves the conservative coefficient
$1/64$; the oracle extension below also proves the paper's sharper
coefficient. The four full targets, model, and transitive model
dependencies were frozen before supporting proofs. The audit checks
all 107 declarations and the exact targets at checkpoint
\nolinkurl{d6feb3069d1a399ab1a50de2b0e7bc13e6b21c23}.

\paragraph{Realizing the magnitude oracles.}
The revealed model grants the entire iid magnitude array, together with an
arbitrary independent random seed, to the policy and output. The selected
model has explicit create, revisit, and stop requests. Each creation may
choose any magnitude in $(0,1/2]$ as a measurable function of the seed and
all previous flips; a revisit names a prior creation. Finite histories
encode coin labels in the common process. The semantic proof shows that
a fresh label has never been used on that path and that a revisited coin
retains its original magnitude. Counterfactual histories do not restrict
magnitudes to a deterministic discrete grid.

Both models use the canonical infinite process and its proved finite-prefix
law, actual expected flip counts, and extended risks. The adversary is one
measurable sign function on the full magnitude domain, including any chosen
magnitudes outside the support of the target law. The estimation target
remains $\int q\,d\mu$. No information inequality or termination premise is
assumed by the final lower bounds; finite budget implies almost-sure stopping.

The clipped hard pair gives KL at most $(128/225)h^2B$ even with the common
magnitude information revealed. The RMS argument gives coefficient $1/64$.
For confidence, clipping at
$h=15\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/B}/(32\sqrt2)$ gives KL at most
$\log(1/\delta)/16$; binary testing and modulus scaling give
$15/(256\sqrt2)$. The original hidden experiment embeds with identical law,
work, risk and confidence events. Zero-work estimators establish nonvacuity.
The models, eight full target types and 33 transitive model dependencies
were frozen at \nolinkurl{3474a37} before the new lower and semantic proofs.
The audit covers ten modules, 54 declaration roots and all eight targets
at \nolinkurl{4a5805c771e541f557848957e0a3ffada31dcb32}.

\paragraph{Realizing the above-two rare-event rate.}
The formal upper construction uses asymmetric, stage-dependent dyadic
boundaries whose wrong-positive error is scaled by $\rho$. Power-tail and
weighted-moment estimates turn the global rarity restriction into a
uniform work bound, and a geometric random-level telescope gives an
integrable unbiased single-coin output with second moment at most
$28\rho$. Its output and work are identified in the actual observable
experiment, and independent fixed-count repetitions or the zero-work
estimator $\rho/2$ meet every budget. The rule is chosen before $\mu$ and
$q$ and uses $\alpha$ only for budget calibration. The lower proof charges
a finite-path information bound to each queried label, controls gaps
below $1/4$ by Bernoulli KL and the remaining range by a variation bound,
and combines a quadratic rare-mixture inequality with the midpoint hybrid
decomposition. The seven full targets were compiled and frozen before
the supporting proofs; the audit covers 54 modules, 322 declaration roots
and all seven targets at
\nolinkurl{637fb4a79d20e30415a2cb0e495752603077c0ee}.

\paragraph{Realizing the critical upper algorithm.}
The formal construction uses asymmetric classifiers at dyadic flip counts,
an alternative to the likelihood-bank construction in \cref{app:critical}.
An independent geometric level selects two adjacent classifiers on one
coin's shared prefix. Their scaled difference, with the base classifier
at level zero, has mean $\theta$, extended second moment at most $28\rho$,
and expected actual flips at most $W=112000000LH$.
An average of $n+1$ independent fresh-coin calls therefore has MSE at most
$28\rho/(n+1)$ and expected work at most $(n+1)W$.

The measurable controller requests only observed flips, stops each call
from its prefix, and realizes these exact cost/output quantities in the
original experiment. For $B\ge W/\rho$, it uses
$\lfloor B/(2W)\rfloor+1$ calls; otherwise it returns zero without flipping.
Both regimes satisfy the budget and the RMS bound with coefficient $100000$.
Neither hidden magnitudes nor signs enter the policy. The full upper target
was frozen before this construction. The Psi model and full types are
separately hash-locked: the formula had a checked draft at freezing, while
the rate and separation targets were fixed before their proofs.

\paragraph{The unbiased converse.}
The formal proof bounds the separation of two expectations by symmetric
KL divergence and their variances. It uses the sign laws
$q_\pm(d)=1/2\pm\varepsilon\min\{1,h/d\}$, charges information to actual
flips in the infinite experiment, and sends $\varepsilon$ to zero. This
gives $\omega_\mu(h)^2\le16h^2V\overline W$. Monotone convergence of
truncated inverse gaps then proves both integrability and the lower product
bound; finite $I_\mu$ is not assumed. The exact target has no KL or
differentiability premise. The model and target were written and compiled
before the supporting proofs; the dependency snapshot was recorded after
the proof drafts. This unbiased converse is for the hidden-magnitude model;
the new oracle results above concern the RMS and confidence lower bounds.

\paragraph{Realizing the 713 estimator.}
A geometric natural-number seed selects a fine classifier and its adjacent
coarse classifier on the same coin. Monotonicity of their stopping levels
means that the coarse output is already determined by the fine classifier's
observed prefix. The controller requests coin zero until that stopping rule
fires, then returns the scaled difference (the base output at level zero).
Every seed and tape has finite actual work, bounded by its dyadic cap.
No hidden magnitude, sign, or gap law enters the policy or output.

The pointwise execution proof identifies the entire stopped transcript,
output and flip count. The existing population simulation law transfers
the conditional Bernoulli analysis to the original adaptive experiment.
Integrating over every measurable sign law gives unbiasedness and the
separate bounds $23I_\mu$ and $31I_\mu$. Extended variance retains the
possibility of infinite values until square integrability is proved.
The two full targets and their transitive model definitions were compiled
and frozen before this controller and its supporting proofs. The audit
checks both targets and all 47 new declaration roots.

\paragraph{Numerical evidence and its limits.}
Exact finite certificates, floating-point recursions, and held-out
simulations do not certify the continuum theorems or the iterated-log
separation. Finite-grid constants do not replace 713: the grid-winning
logarithmic threshold coefficient $1/2$ fails the uniform error envelope
at an explicit large cap, whereas this construction retains coefficient
one. The finite-budget feedback advantage and practical experiments
remain supporting research with their original limited scopes.

\paragraph{Provenance and review.}
This version incorporates the original law, the 713 argument, and the
rare-event continuations, with full proofs in the main text and
\cref{app:localization,app:above-two,app:critical}. It adds the
general-law rare-event manuscript (revised v2) in
\cref{sec:entropy-tail,sec:certificates,sec:related-work} and
\cref{sec:entropy-tail-upper,sec:matching-lower,sec:uf-foundations,sec:lower-foundations,sec:transfer-foundations,sec:flow-foundations,sec:exact-dual,sec:midpoint-dual,sec:genie,sec:identifiability,sec:computation},
and the finite-gap rare-event manuscript (revision 2) in
\cref{fg:sec:model} and \cref{app:finite-direct}. Proofs that appeared
in both rare-event manuscripts in substantially identical form appear
once: the shared appendices follow the general-law version, with the
finite-gap manuscript's clarifications added, and the finite-gap upper
bound cites the general construction. The two proofs of the finite-law
rate share that construction and those appendices, so they are not
independent confirmations.
The assembly script checks source identities and records editorial
changes; historical sources and reviews remain unchanged.
AI agents performed the work under David Iach's direction. No external
specialist review or model diversity is established. One critical
reviewer authored part of the argument, precluding two independent
validations of the full proof. The
\href{https://github.com/davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation/blob/6009662a5581b31de17c8956b6fd93e4279ba087/docs/process/METHOD.md}{process note}
records the workflow, failed approaches, and these limits.

\paragraph{Remaining mathematical work.}
The entropy--tail law, the closed quantile form, the stopping-program
results, the rare-event regimes below two, and auxiliary envelope,
smoothness, and observed-state results retain their stated paper-proof
scopes; their formalization is the next verification target. Sharp
comparison constants, efficient selection for structured laws, rates of
cap convergence, and sharp rare-event confidence rates at and above the
quadratic boundary remain open. The rare-event results assume a known
magnitude law; unknown laws, other observation kernels and almost-sure
budgets require separate arguments.

The model requires independent fresh coins and conditionally independent
flips, without drift or additional smooth structure linking states.
Expected-work optimality supplies no deterministic latency bound or
data-computable confidence radius over all unknown laws. Neither useful
finite critical constants nor practical superiority is established.
