\section*{Addendum: all finite gap counts and explicit base costs}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{All finite gap counts and explicit base costs}

This extension supplements the preserved critical-law proof. For the same
observable $1/10$-correct sign test and separated gaps $0<d_j\le1/128$,
with finite expected work under both signs, every integer $K\ge1$ satisfies
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^K d_j^2\E_{-d_j}T\ge\frac{K\log K}{4096}.
\]
The existing coefficient $1/512$ still applies when $\log K\ge32$.
Thus the restriction on the number of gaps is removed with a uniform
coefficient eight times smaller. The all-$K$ extension with coefficient
$1/512$ is not established here.

\paragraph{The finite-prefix floor.}
Fix one gap $d$ and put $n=\lfloor1/(32d^2)\rfloor$. The negative and
positive laws of the seed and first $n$ flips have divergence at most
\[
 \frac{4nd^2}{(1/2+d)(1/2-d)}\le\frac8{15},
\]
since $d\le1/128<1/8$. Let $E$ be the event that the test stops by time
$n$ and returns minus. Its positive-law probability is at most $1/10$.
If its negative-law probability exceeded $5/8$, binary data processing
and Pinsker would give divergence at least
$2(5/8-1/10)^2=441/800>8/15$. Consequently
\[
 \Pp_{-d}(T>n)\ge1-1/10-5/8=11/40,
 \qquad
 d^2\E_{-d}T\ge\frac{11}{40}\left(\frac1{32}-\frac1{16384}\right)
 >\frac1{128}.
\]
Only finite expected work under the negative law is used for this floor;
fair-coin termination is unnecessary. Summing gives $K/128$. For
$\log K\le32$ this dominates $K\log K/4096$; for $\log K\ge32$ the
original entropy theorem (BC2 in the preserved critical-law argument)
supplies the stronger coefficient $1/512$.

\subsection*{Explicit base costs for the same single-coin model}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Explicit single-coin base costs}
Write $L=1+\log(1/\rho)$, $H=1+\log L$, and $W_0=\E_0T$.
For any observable single-coin estimator with uniform bias at most
$\rho/64$ on $\mathcal X_\rho$, finite work under each prior, and a
uniform raw second moment bound $M$, the new proof gives
\[
 M W_0\ge 10^{-10}\rho LH.
\]
If instead $V$ bounds the extended variance uniformly, it gives
\[
 V W_0\ge\frac{\rho LH}{15\cdot10^9}.
\]
These statements retain the original quantifiers over seed spaces,
observable procedures, and priors; conditional moments and repeated
sign tests are constructed inside the proof.

Here is the constant calculation. Put $P=MW_0$. Activation still gives
$P\ge\rho/6$. The all-size entropy inequality and the existing good-gap
selection give, whenever all selected annuli have mass at least $560P$,
\[
 \rho K\log K\le(21004288/3)P.
\]
For $t=\log(1/\rho)\ge16384$ choose
$K=\lfloor t/(8\log16)\rfloor$. If $P\ge e^{-t/2}$, the existing
exponential bound already gives $P\ge\rho LH$. Otherwise the annulus
mass condition holds and $LH\le256\log16\,K\log K$.
Since $256\log16\,(21004288/3)<10^{10}$, this proves the raw-moment claim.
For $t<16384$, $LH\le16385^2<10^{10}/6$, so the activation floor suffices.
The variance claim follows from the existing bound $M\le3V/2$ in the
uniform-bias reduction.

The earlier constructive proof used
$t_0=8192+16\log16+(16\log16)^2+16\log16\,e^{32}$ and a raw-moment
constant about $1.29\cdot10^{-18}$. The new explicit constant is about
$7.7\cdot10^7$ times larger. This numerical comparison concerns the
constants produced by these proofs, not the optimal constant. No explicit
constant is propagated through the common-pair and population-minimax
steps here, and no practical improvement for an estimator is inferred.
