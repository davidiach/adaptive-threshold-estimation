\begin{abstract}
We verify a sharp critical-margin obstruction for single-coin estimators
and investigate its population implications. For the exact quadratic
magnitude law on $(0,1/4]$, separate uniform variance and expected-work
suprema have product of order $\rho L(1+\log L)$, where
$L=1+\log(1/\rho)$, even with uniform bias at most $\rho/64$.
This result was inherited from concurrent repository work; the current
checkpoint independently audits it and refines one proof. It excludes
budget-dependent raw iid averages but does not exclude nonlinear population
estimators. A finite-prefix directional-information reduction handles
arbitrary creation and interleaving of coins. An explicit observable
gambler's-ruin procedure disproves the extra-log information upper bound
on the natural radial submodel; calibration attains the rate without that
logarithm on the specified submodel. A separate fixed-feature theorem
excludes improvement from independent pilot selection of a scalar control
variate. Exact rational finite-horizon diagnostics exhibit small benefits
from adaptive allocation. The arbitrary-law rare-event minimax problem
remains unresolved.
\end{abstract}

The finite computations are \textbf{Numerical}, using exact rational

\section{Experiment and question}
For each fresh iid coin, $D\sim\mu$ on $(0,1/2]$ is hidden and
$S\mid D=d\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q(d))$. Conditional flips are iid
with success probability $1/2+(2S-1)D$. The unknown measurable allocation
belongs to the global class
$\mathcal Q_\rho=\{0\le q\le1:\int q\,d\mu\le\rho\}$;
the target is $\theta(q)=\int q\,d\mu$. Knowing $\mu,\rho$ does not reveal
any coin's $D,S$. Algorithms observe bits and coin identities, may create,
revisit and interleave coins, and may stop adaptively. Every genuine flip
costs one, including pilot flips. Deterministic arithmetic and independent
randomness are free. Exact deterministic integrals of the specified known
law are allowed in theoretical constructions; approximate implementations
need a separate numerical error analysis. Uniform expected work is
$\sup_q\E_qT\le B$, not a deterministic cap.

The candidate arbitrary-law functional is
\[
 \Psi_{\mu,\rho}(h)=\sup_{0\le w\le1,\ \int w\,d\mu\le\rho}
           \int\min\{w(d),h\sqrt{w(d)}/d\}\,\mu(dd).
\]
For the exact quadratic law with $a=1/4$, its unsaturated order is
$\sqrt{\rho L/B}$ at $h=B^{-1/2}$; the currently established upper
construction for arbitrary $q$ retains the factor $\sqrt{1+\log L}$.
The single-coin obstruction alone does not decide which rate is the full
population minimax rate. Confidence-radius optimality and uniform
adaptation to unknown $\mu,\rho$ are not established in this checkpoint.

\section{Exact finite-horizon evidence}
Two preregistered synthetic laws have respectively masses
$(1/8,7/8)$ at gaps $(1/32,1/4)$ and masses $(1/16,3/16,3/4)$ at gaps
$(1/32,1/8,1/2)$. In both cases $\rho=1/8$. The finite prior is uniform
over $q=0$, feasible single-atom allocations with target $\rho/2$ or
$\rho$, and $q\equiv\rho$, with duplicates removed. The prior draws one
common $q$ before generating any coins. Neither sampled signs nor gaps
are observed.

At deterministic horizons one through eight we solve the finite Bayes
allocation problem exactly with rational arithmetic. A state is the sorted
list of heads/tails counts for each observed coin. Transcript likelihoods
factor by coin; the terminal posterior mean is optimal, and minimizing
the two-child expected posterior variance over fresh/revisited choices
gives the Bellman recursion. Randomization and earlier stopping cannot
improve the Bayes minimum. A separate labeled ordered-tape recursion agrees
exactly at all ten case/horizon pairs through horizon five.

\section{Exact remaining obligations}
A full resolution needs either a biased or nonlinear population estimator
with uniform error and expected work at the candidate scale for every
global allocation, or a population lower bound exploiting nuisance
directions beyond the radial path. The current product lower bound does
not apply after clipping or nonlinear aggregation. The pilot theorem fixes
its production features and excludes adaptive acquisition. The information
reduction supplies no automatic bound on its one-coin directional premise.
Finite computations do not close any of these logical gaps. Extending
the result to arbitrary measures, joint rarity/accuracy/confidence limits,
or unknown-law adaptation remains separate work.
