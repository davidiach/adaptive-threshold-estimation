## Checks of the new lower proof

1. **Admissible class.** One genuine hidden coin is used throughout; independent parameter-free simulations may be part of the seed. The policy and output are fixed across every q in the global rarity class. They may depend on μ,a,ρ but not on the realized D,S or unknown q. Zero-observation stopping is allowed. The conclusion is about the infimum of separate uniform variance and work products, without an attainment claim.

2. **Conditional expectations and response.** The two admissible populations q=0 and q≡ρ yield finite integrated conditional second moments and work for both signs. A single common null set suffices; no intersection over uncountably many q is needed. Integrability permits the signed subtraction using q=min(1,ρ/μ(E))1_E. Since its coefficient is positive whenever μ(E)>0, this gives ∫_E(m_+−m_−)=μ(E), hence m_+−m_−=1 almost everywhere. The argument uses the integral rarity constraint correctly.

3. **Raw moments versus variance.** Conditional Jensen at q≡ρ, together with ∫m_−=0, gives E_ρY²≥ρ. Therefore V≥ρ(1−ρ), M≥ρ, and M≤V+ρ²≤(4/3)V. These inequalities use suprema only as upper bounds and do not require an attained supremum. The lower proof does not mistake a product for a product of separate suprema.

4. **Active normalization and zero-cost edge.** A={T≥1} is seed-measurable before any observation, with a common probability p>0 for all latent states. Its complementary expectation c is independent of q. Conditioning the seed on A and returning Z=pY+c preserves unbiasedness. The exact identity E_qZ²=p E_q[Y²1_A]+2cθ−c² and the lower bound E[Y²1_(Aᶜ)]≥c²/(1−p) give E_qZ²≤pM+(1−p)θ². This handles arbitrary artificial thinning, including signed zero-cost outputs. The p=1 case is separately covered. In particular pM≥ρ(1−ρ), m=pM+ρ²≤(4/3)pM, W_Z=W/p≥1, and mW_Z≤(4/3)MW. There is no division by zero or hidden cost from genuine flips during seed resampling.

5. **Good-gap selection.** Markov gives μ{M_−>1/64}≤64m. If {M_+−M_−>2m/ρ} had mass at least ρ, nonatomicity supplies a subset of mass exactly ρ and an admissible allocation contradicting the uniform moment bound. Thus at most another ρ≤m of gap mass is removed. On the remaining set, the negative mean has magnitude at most 1/8 and the positive mean is at least 7/8. The positive second moment is bounded by 1/64+2m/ρ. This selection is existential in the proof, not information supplied to the algorithm.

6. **Same-coin restarts.** With n=ceil(256(1+m/ρ))≤513m/ρ, the repeated active outputs at a fixed good latent state are iid using fresh independent seeds and unused successive tape blocks. Their conditional stopping times are finite almost surely because their conditional means are finite. Countable conditioning on the finite stopped prefixes justifies the suffix property. Chebyshev at the common threshold 1/2 gives the stated error below 1/10, with expected negative work exactly n w_−(d). No fresh latent coin is drawn in this sign reduction.

7. **Finite-grid sign entropy lemma.** The early-time KL is at most (64/3)n_jd_j²≤1/48, hence its total variation is below 1/8. Combining correctness, that early-event comparison, and Markov at u_j=ceil(4E_−τ) puts probability greater than 1/4 in the stated finite window. KL against the fair prefix is at most 16c_j+1; binary data processing yields P₀(A_j)≥exp(−64c_j−8). These are deterministic finite-prefix comparisons. No termination or optional-stopping claim under the fair coin is used.

8. **Entropy overlap and constants.** For at least K/2 indices, c_j≤2c̄. The corresponding time windows have endpoints proportional to d_j⁻², with ratio at most (16c̄+2)/b₀. Since those scales are separated by at least four, their overlap is at most 1+log₄((16c̄+2)/b₀). The same fair-coin path has only one terminal time; infinite terminal time belongs to no window. The summed fair probabilities are therefore bounded by this overlap. The displayed inequality implies c̄≥log(K)/512 for K≥ceil(exp(32)); the numerical inequalities used in the contradiction are loose but valid.

9. **Continuous-law annuli.** I_j=(a4^(−j)/2,a4^(−j)] has mass (3/4)16^(−j). The chosen upper index J ensures every retained annulus has mass at least 130m, leaving at least half its mass good. Selecting w_− at no more than four times the full-annulus average is valid. Its gap square is at most a²16^(−j), giving the aggregate factor 16a²/3. Active procedures always take at least one observation, so a zero conditional-work integral cannot create a spurious selected zero-work sign test. The chosen gaps have the required separation and upper endpoint below 1/128.

10. **Product conversion and all rarity levels.** The coefficient (16/3)·513·(4/3)=3648 is correct. If P=MW<sqrt(ρ), active normalization forces m<(4/3)sqrt(ρ), leaving K bounded below by a constant times log(1/ρ) for sufficiently small universal ρ. The finite-grid inequality then forces P≥cρL(1+log L). If P≥sqrt(ρ), that order bound holds directly at sufficiently small ρ. On the remaining compact rarity interval, P≥3ρ/4 supplies a positive universal constant after decreasing c. Fixing a=1/4 makes every threshold universal. Finally M≤(4/3)V transfers the lower order to VW.

## What the theorem establishes and what it does not

The lower proof applies to arbitrary observable single-coin procedures with all-q unbiasedness and finite separate uniform moments, including arbitrary random stopping and all variance/work tradeoffs. It is substantially stronger than failure of the likelihood dictionary or its proof certificate. The previously available upper construction matches its order in that class.

The essential premise is the pointwise conditional response identity forced by single-coin all-q unbiasedness. An arbitrary biased population estimator does not have that identity for one genuine coin. This review therefore does not transfer the new lower bound to the full fixed-budget minimax class. The current theorem neither refutes the candidate critical Ψ rate nor proves that rate achievable. A proposed projection or reduction must establish observable implementation and cost before being used to bridge that gap.

The constants are deliberately extremely loose and the proof's first asymptotic threshold is very small. This affects quantitative usefulness, not the stated order theorem with unspecified universal constants. Scientific novelty remains a separate literature question; the finite-scale sign-entropy mechanism is explicitly credited to prior work and is re-proved here for the needed Bernoulli setting.
