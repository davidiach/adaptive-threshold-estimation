# Critical quadratic margin: focused primary-source refresh

This refresh addresses the remaining scale-discovery issue at margin exponent two. It supplements, rather than repeats, [the preceding source audit](../2026-10-27-route-a/sources.md). It is an agent source comparison, not external specialist review or a novelty certificate. The reviewer does not author the construction under review; model diversity among agents is not guaranteed.

## Finite scale entropy

**Chen, Li and Qiao, arXiv:1608.06031v2 (2017), _Towards Instance Optimal Bounds for Best Arm Identification_.** [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1608.06031v2).

Inspected Definition 1.4, Lemma 4.1, Appendix D's proof and its Bernoulli extension. For a delta-correct SIGN test, delta<0.04, define a_k=4^(-k) E_(2^-k) T. For every probability vector p on finitely many dyadic gaps, Lemma 4.1 gives sum p_k a_k >= c[Ent(p)+log(1/delta)]. The stated experiment is unit-variance Gaussian; Remark D.4 includes interior Bernoulli means via quadratic KL. The proof transfers finite stopping-window events to the zero-gap law and selects disjoint windows. Crucially, correctness is used only at each finite pair of opposite gaps; zero-gap correctness or termination is not used. Only the displayed one-sided expected costs enter. These proof facts support a finite-pair application, although perturbed geometric gaps require an explicit adaptation rather than a verbatim theorem citation. The best-arm corollary distinguishes a sub-instance result from an all-instance bound for monotone algorithms. This is a classifier obstruction, not a lower bound for arbitrary population-mass estimators; no reduction from the latter task has been established here.

## Iterated logarithms in sequential testing

**Jamieson, Malloy, Nowak and Bubeck (2014), _lil' UCB_.** [Primary paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/LilUCB.pdf).

Inspected Section 2, especially Theorem 1's explicit restatement of Farrell and Corollary 1. For Gaussian observations N(Delta,1), a single sign test with error at most delta uniformly over every nonzero Delta satisfies limsup_(Delta->0) E_Delta T/[Delta^-2 log log Delta^-2] >= 2-4delta. Both the uniform correctness requirement and the limsup are essential. The result does not give this lower bound at every gap or an integrated lower bound for the quadratic margin. The paper's own best-arm theorem concerns a high-probability sample bound; it should not be silently read as the expected-work population theorem sought here.

**Shin, Ramdas and Rinaldo, arXiv:2010.08082, May 2021 version, _Nonparametric iterated-logarithm extensions of the sequential generalized likelihood ratio test_.** [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.08082).

Inspected Theorems 2 and 3, equations (35)--(56), and the stopping/overshoot argument in Appendix B. Theorem 2 bounds the expected stopping time of an open-ended SGLR test with separated hypotheses; in the sub-Gaussian case it is at most 2 sigma^2[g_alpha+2]/(mu-mu0)^2+1. Its boundary depends on the prescribed alternative separation. Theorem 3 and equation (56) give an adaptive expected-time bound with an iterated logarithm in the inverse divergence. Equations (44)--(45) restate Farrell's exponential-family limsup obstruction. These are tests of one unknown conditional mean, not estimators of a hidden reservoir's sign mass with an integrated rarity restriction. Their time-uniform likelihood methods overlap the classifier ingredients; they neither prove nor refute the desired population minimax rate.

## Existing coin-mixture and debiasing inputs

**Lee--Valiant, [arXiv:1904.09228v3](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.09228v3).** Corollary 19 and Lemma 18 were audited in the preceding run; this pass does not claim a second full replay of that input. Reinspected Theorems 4--5, Sections 7.1--7.2, Lemmas 22--23 and their optimization objectives. The estimator's two average-response constraints use fixed known conditional laws h+/h-, a design mixture weight and a hard per-coin depth cap. Their comparison with fully adaptive procedures varies that scalar mixture weight while preserving those conditional laws. It does not produce one policy with the pointwise response required by every nuisance allocation q here. As an elementary model check, preserving a fixed unconditional magnitude law at two distinct mixture weights forces the two fixed conditional magnitude laws to equal each other and mu; that path has constant q. An extension to changing conditional laws or multidimensional nuisance directions requires new reasoning, as does removal of the depth cap. No cost-preserving conversion for the present all-q model was established. Also, their minimum-gap theorem cannot replace a positive minimum gap by the maximum support point a.

The earlier audit of Giles--Haji-Ali and Haji-Ali--Spence--Teckentrup already records adaptive nested probability estimation and critical MLMC complexity. The earlier Vihola audit records both single-term randomized telescoping and Poisson replication. Removing a scale-discovery factor in the present observation model would require additional mathematics beyond those established allocation devices.

**Brennan, Vinayak and Jamieson (ICML 2020), _Estimating the Number and Effect Sizes of Non-null Hypotheses_.** [Primary paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/brennan20a/brennan20a.pdf).

Followed this explicit Lee--Valiant reference and inspected the sampling model, Theorem 2.1 with its proof, Corollary 2.1.2, Lemma 2.2 and Proposition 3.1. Theorem 2.1 uses iid latent effects and one noisy statistic per effect; its conservative simultaneous tail-mass guarantee depends on CDF separation from competing mixture distributions. The Gaussian two-spike specialization has a relative-estimation sample bound proportional to sigma^4/(zeta^2 gamma^4), and Lemma 2.2 gives a corresponding local estimation lower bound. Proposition 3.1 analyzes uniform replication in a pilot study. These are genuine threshold-mass estimation results, not merely discovery identification, but they do not provide the present adaptive one-coin variance/work characterization or a reduction for arbitrary hidden q at fixed quadratic magnitude law. The supplemental proof of Lemma 2.2 was not rederived here. This limited check does not certify that the broader multiple-testing literature lacks a predecessor.

## Finite-trigger and Bernoulli-factory overlap

**Nacu and Peres (2005), _Fast simulation of new coins from old_, arXiv:math/0309222v5.** [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0309222).

Inspected the introduction's equation (1), Section 2 and the necessary direction of Proposition 3. The introduction restates Keane--O'Brien's simulation criterion, including a polynomial lower envelope away from probabilities zero and one. Section 2 expresses finite-output events as polynomials with nonnegative Bernoulli-word coefficients. This is direct established overlap with the elementary finite-trigger positivity lemma in `alternative.md`. The original Keane--O'Brien paper was not inspected in this pass, and no new Bernoulli-factory theorem is claimed. None of these observations rules out simulators tailored to finite gap sets separated from zero or establishes a population-estimation lower bound.
