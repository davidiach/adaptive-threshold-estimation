# Failed approaches and precise limits

1. **Small bias as the only escape from the single-coin product.** Uniform bias<=rho/64 still forces the critical H factor. A B-dependent raw iid average does not evade it. This is proved, not an empirical failure. Clipping and nonlinear aggregation are not ruled out.
2. **Project a general population estimator to one coin and apply that product.** The projection may depend on an infinite tape, have no finite-work observable realization, and only control moments at one reference law. Small uniform bias does not control derivatives. No valid general reduction was obtained. Those gaps are not evidence that the unrestricted theorem is false.
3. **Transfer the extra log through the natural radial Fisher direction.** Analytically disproved: an observable finite-cap envelope has information/work>=a^2rho/(32L). A calibrated estimator attains the corresponding no-H rate on that known radial family. Multiple nuisance directions might still matter in the full model.
4. **Use an independent pilot only to learn a scalar control-variate coefficient for fixed unbiased features.** A saddle point supplies one q_star bad for every coefficient; pilot learning cannot improve its worst-case raw MSE. The explicit polynomial features also have poor variance constants. Pilot-dependent acquisition, feature selection and nonlinear output remain outside this theorem.
5. **Read exact finite-cap Bayes optima as unrestricted minimax evidence.** Different parameter class, objective and work constraint. The finite results show allocation effects only; they cannot settle expected-budget minimax.
6. **Treat Tier5 completion as the rare-population bridge.** Nine actual targets pass, but RealizesCreationPrefixes and balanced priors are inputs. General rare-reservoir existence and semantic fidelity require another bridge.
