\noindent\textbf{Companion-note status.} The canonical strongest claim and its
current acceptance status are in \texttt{unrestricted.tex}. This note records
the intermediate independent-design result and explains the origin of the
shared affine-estimation fragment used by that full-model proof. It is not
Lean-checked or externally specialist-reviewed; separate agent-review records
accompany the sources. The result here extends the previous additive obstruction
to arbitrary nonlinear aggregation for the two acquisition designs specified
below. All constants are independent of the rarity and budget; the support
endpoint is fixed at $a=1/4$.

\section{Contract and strongest statement}
Let
\[
 \mu(dd)=\frac{2d}{a^2}\mathbf1_{(0,a]}(d)\,dd,\quad a=1/4,
 \qquad \mathcal Q_\rho=\{q:0\le q\le1,\ \int q\,d\mu\le\rho\},
 \quad 0<\rho\le1/4.
\]
Write $\theta(q)=\int q\,d\mu$, $L=1+\log(1/\rho)$, and
$H=1+\log L$. Every coin has hidden $D\sim\mu$ and
$S\mid D=d\sim\operatorname{Ber}(q(d))$; its flips are conditionally iid
with parameter $1/2+(2S-1)D$. A local procedure observes its own flips and
its independent seed only. It may know $\mu,\rho,B$, may stop before any
flip, and may have unbounded stopping time. Nontermination has infinite work.
Seeds and stopped observable transcripts are standard Borel random elements.
This includes countable random tapes and their measurable policies.
All local observations are charged; independent randomization and measurable
terminal calculations are free, as in the original statistical model.

We consider two classes of observation designs, selected without knowing $q$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathsf D$: a deterministic finite number $n\ge0$ of independent
fresh coins, with arbitrary, possibly heterogeneous local procedures and
independent local seeds. Their full stopped transcripts $Z_1,\ldots,Z_n$
are independent conditional on the population $q$. The final output is any
measurable function of these transcripts, with optional independent terminal
randomness. There is no shared random variable selecting the local procedures.
\item $\mathsf P$: a fixed $\lambda\in[0,\infty)$ is chosen, then
$N\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda)$ independently of all coins. The same
fixed local procedure, with independent seeds, is applied to each of the
$N$ coins. Its rule must not depend on the realized $N$. The final output
may be any measurable function of $N$ and all the ordered stopped transcripts,
with optional independent terminal randomness.
\end{enumerate}
In either class the final function may clip, threshold, or otherwise combine
the data nonlinearly. Each local procedure may adapt to its own coin's past,
but no local action can depend on another coin's observations. Independent
execution may be scheduled or interleaved without changing these rules.
Require a uniform expected total flip count at most $B$ and finite uniform
RMS error. The infimum defining $R_{\mathsf D,\rho}(B)$ or
$R_{\mathsf P,\rho}(B)$ is over all such designs and terminal outputs in
the indicated class; no component moment restriction is imposed on the input
design beyond its finite uniform work. In class $\mathsf D$ only the final
output needs finite uniform RMS. The count-zero cases give seed-only outputs.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp RMS law with arbitrary nonlinear aggregation]\label{thm:main}
There are universal constants $0<c<C<\infty$ such that, for every
$0<\rho\le1/4$ and $B\ge1$, and for each
$\mathsf C\in\{\mathsf D,\mathsf P\}$,
\[
 c\min\left\{\rho,\sqrt{\frac{\rho LH}{B}}\right\}
 \le R_{\mathsf C,\rho}(B)
 \le C\min\left\{\rho,\sqrt{\frac{\rho LH}{B}}\right\}.       \tag{1}
\]
This characterizes an infimum up to constants, not attainment of an exact
optimal procedure. In particular, arbitrary clipping of an estimator in
either class cannot remove the factor $\sqrt H$ in the nonsaturated regime.
\end{theorem}

For the present law the proposed unrestricted minimax modulus satisfies
$\Psi_{\mu,\rho}(B^{-1/2})\asymp
\min\{\rho,\sqrt{\rho L/B}\}$, with $a=1/4$ absorbed in the constants.
Thus (1) has an unbounded extra factor $\sqrt H$ when $\rho\downarrow0$
and, for example, $B=\rho^{-2}$. Any procedure attaining the proposed
full-model rate must escape both stated acquisition classes. This direct
comparison alone does not rule out shared random selection of a local
experiment or cross-coin feedback; the canonical note uses the stronger
universal-pair argument to address that gap.

\section{Full stopped transcripts and the convex parameter image}
For a fixed local procedure, denote by $K_{d,s}$ its stopped transcript law
at hidden state $(d,s)$. Its observable transcript includes its private
seed, stopping time, and observed finite bit string. For every measurable
transcript event $A$,
\[
 P_q(A)=\int\big[(1-q(d))K_{d,0}(A)+q(d)K_{d,1}(A)\big]\,d\mu. \tag{6}
\]
All kernels in (6) are measurable. They are determined by the fixed local
policy, without observing its latent state. Let $P_*=P_{q_*}$ for the
admissible constant function $q_*(d)=\rho$. Then
\[
                 P_q\le\rho^{-1}P_* \quad(q\in\mathcal Q_\rho). \tag{7}
\]
This follows directly from $q\le1$, $1-q\le1$ and $1-\rho\ge\rho$;
it does not require $q(d)\le\rho$. Finite expected work at $q_*$ implies
almost-sure finite stopping there, and (7) implies the same for all $q$.

Every transcript cell with positive $P_*$ probability has strictly positive
probability under every admissible $q$. To verify this without an
unproved equivalence claim for infinite product laws, decompose that cell
by its finite stopping time $t$ and bit string $x\in\{0,1\}^t$.
For the seed set causing this stopped record to belong to the cell, let
$b_{t,x}$ be its seed probability. At a fixed Bernoulli parameter $p$ the
cell probability is
\[
 \sum_{t,x} b_{t,x}p^{\#1(x)}(1-p)^{t-\#1(x)}.
\]
Some $b_{t,x}>0$, since the cell has positive baseline probability.
All possible $p$ lie in $[1/4,3/4]$, so this single term is at least
$b_{t,x}4^{-t}>0$, uniformly in the latent state and in $q$.

For any finite list of finite measurable transcript partitions, discard
baseline-null cells, which are null for every $q$ by (7). Take the vector
$x(q)$ of all remaining cell probabilities together with $\theta(q)$.
Its range $X$ is finite-dimensional compact convex. In detail,
$\mathcal Q_\rho$ is weak-star compact in $L^\infty(\mu)$ by
Banach--Alaoglu: the box $0\le q\le1$ and the integral inequality are
weak-star closed. Each coordinate in (6), and $\theta$, is a weak-star
continuous affine functional, since its kernel belongs to $L^1(\mu)$.
Thus the image is compact convex, each probability coordinate is strictly
positive throughout it, and $\theta$ is its linear last coordinate.
Distinct functions $q$ with the same probability coordinates may have
different last coordinates; this causes no difficulty and does not assume
identifiability. This verifies all parameter conditions of
Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison} for the finite partitions actually used.

\section{Fixed independent heterogeneous designs}
Fix a design in $\mathsf D$ with $n\ge1$, and any terminal output of worst
RMS $r$. First project its output onto $[0,\rho]$, which decreases squared
error for every $\theta\in[0,\rho]$. Average out independent terminal
randomization. We obtain a bounded measurable function $G(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)$
of no larger RMS. This operation leaves every local procedure unchanged.

By independence and (7), its transcript law satisfies
\[
        \bigotimes_{i=1}^nP_{i,q}
          \le \rho^{-n}\bigotimes_{i=1}^nP_{i,*}.            \tag{8}
\]
On a finite product probability space, simple functions measurable with
respect to finite product partitions are dense in $L^2$. For example,
finite linear combinations of measurable rectangles form a dense class,
and their component sets have a common finite partition refinement.
Choose such a function $G_\varepsilon$ with baseline squared error small
enough that (8) implies
\[
 \sup_q\|G_\varepsilon-G\|_{L^2(P_q)}\le\varepsilon.
\]
One may clip the approximation as well. Hence $G_\varepsilon$ has worst
RMS at most $r+\varepsilon$. It depends only on the finitely partitioned
individual stopped transcripts. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison} to their
convex probability image: an output
$F_\varepsilon=c+\sum_i\phi_i(Z_i)$ has worst RMS at most
$12(r+\varepsilon)+\varepsilon$.

Each $\phi_i$ is a function of a finite partition, hence bounded. It is
observable at the original local stopping time, with exactly the original
local work. Thus all separate component moment conditions in the inherited
additive lower bound hold. No extra coin or flip was used. That lower bound
gives
\[
 12r+13\varepsilon\ge
 c_0\min\{\rho,\sqrt{\rho LH/B}\}.
\]
Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The comparison constructs finite approximants;
it makes no unjustified assertion that a limiting additive procedure is
realized or has finite work. If $n=0$, the output law is independent of $q$,
and comparing $q=0$ with $q=\rho$ gives worst RMS at least $\rho/2$.
This proves the lower bound of (1) for $\mathsf D$.

\section{Independent Poisson replication}
Fix $\lambda>0$ and a design in $\mathsf P$ with local mark law $P_q$.
Again clip the final output to $[0,\rho]$ and average out terminal randomness.
For each count $n$, average this function over all $n!$ permutations of the
ordered marks. The marks are iid conditional on $N=n$, so Jensen's inequality
shows that this symmetrization cannot increase risk. Denote the resulting
bounded symmetric function by $G$.

Use the reference experiment $N_*\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda/\rho)$
with iid marks of law $P_*$. Under this reference, the likelihood ratio of
the actual count-and-mark law at $q$ is
\[
 e^{\lambda/\rho-\lambda}\rho^n
       \prod_{i=1}^n\frac{dP_q}{dP_*}(z_i)
       \le e^{\lambda/\rho-\lambda}.                       \tag{9}
\]
The constant can be large but is finite for each fixed design; no uniform
efficiency of the finite approximation is asserted or needed.

For any $\varepsilon>0$, $G$ can be approximated in reference $L^2$ by a
bounded function of the cell counts of one finite measurable mark
partition. Here is a direct justification. First ignore the reference tail
$N_*>m$, whose contribution vanishes because $G$ is bounded. For each of
the finitely many counts $n\le m$, approximate $G$ in $L^2(P_*^n)$ by a
finite product-partition function. Refine all the finitely many component
partitions to one common finite mark partition and symmetrize each
approximation. Its value then depends only on the mark cell counts.
Set it to zero for $N>m$. This is still a function of the same finite cell
counts, since their sum is $N$. Inequality (9) upgrades an arbitrarily
accurate reference approximation to
$\sup_q\|G_\varepsilon-G\|_2\le\varepsilon$.

For a finite partition $A_1,\ldots,A_k$ with positive baseline cells, the
actual counts $C_1,\ldots,C_k$ are independent Poisson variables with
intensities $\lambda P_q(A_j)$. These are strictly positive affine
coordinates on the compact convex signal image of the previous section.
Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison} gives a count-affine output
\[
                  F_\varepsilon=c+\sum_{j=1}^k\beta_j C_j
\]
with RMS at most $12r+13\varepsilon$. On the original experiment this is
$c+\sum_{i=1}^N\beta_{j(Z_i)}$, an additive estimator with exactly the same
work. Its expected component square sum is at most
$\lambda\max_j\beta_j^2<\infty$ and its uniform second moment is finite
because $\E N^2<\infty$. The inherited additive lower bound applies;
letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves (1)'s lower bound for $\mathsf P$.
For $\lambda=0$, the earlier seed-only lower bound applies.

\section{Matching upper bounds and realization}
Use the inherited unbiased local kernel $Y,T$ with the uniform constants
$M_0=C_0\rho$, $W_0=C_1LH$ fixed above. For class $\mathsf D$, choose
$n=\lceil B/W_0\rceil\ge1$ and $p=B/(nW_0)\in(0,1]$.
Independently for each of the $n$ coins, activate the kernel with probability
$p$, stopping before any observation on the other event, and return
$A_iY_i/(np)$. The sum is unbiased and obeys
\[
 \sup_q\E_q\sum_i A_iT_i\le npW_0=B,\qquad
 \sup_q\Var_q\left(\sum_i\frac{A_iY_i}{np}\right)
       \le\frac{M_0}{np}=\frac{M_0W_0}{B}.                 \tag{10}
\]
This is a deterministic finite design with private activation seeds.
For class $\mathsf P$, choose $\lambda=B/W_0$ and output
$\lambda^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N Y_i$. The elementary Poisson mean and variance
identities give the same bounds as (10). The kernel is fixed before $N$
is drawn. In either class choose the constant output $\rho/2$ instead when
it gives a smaller bound. This proves the upper half of (1) for every
budget, including the saturation regime.

No latent-variable access is hidden in these constructions. A probability
space may realize each coin by independent uniforms via
$D=a\sqrt{U_0}$, $S=\mathbf1_{\{U_1\le q(D)\}}$, and subsequent
Bernoulli flips from independent uniforms; the policy sees only its own
independent seed and flips. The inherited random-level local kernel has
genuinely unbounded finite-mean stopping, as proved in the pinned source.
Our comparison retains these full stopped observable records and their
work; it does not replace them with an oracle magnitude or a bounded
horizon. All null sets discarded in the argument are simultaneously null
for every admissible $q$ by (7) or (9).

\section{Adversarial checks, limitations, and next question}
The distinction between marginal affinity and the whole product is
essential: each $P_{i,q}$ is affine in $q$, but
$\bigotimes_iP_{i,q}$ is usually not. The proof uses the additive
log-moment structure, not a false assertion of product affinity.
It also uses the global constraint $\int q\le\rho$, not a pointwise rarity
bound. The reference $q_*=\rho$ is only an analytic dominating law and is
never supplied as the true population to a procedure.

A shared random choice of the entire local experiment can destroy the
required log-moment concavity. Accordingly a general seed-dependent
preselected design or count is not covered by the new comparison, even
though it is covered by the earlier \emph{additive} lower theorem.
Nor is acquisition using other coins' outcomes covered by this direct
comparison. This was the exact gap that motivated the stronger base-cost
and universal-pair route, now developed in \texttt{unrestricted.tex}. The
population-step radial construction already demonstrates that a natural
one-dimensional radial direction does not carry the extra logarithm;
this note makes no contrary directional-information claim.

The parameter-image and finite-partition steps are existence arguments
for measurable statistical algorithms. They do not assert that a finite
convex program can compute the required partitions from a source-code
description of an arbitrary policy. Computational work is not charged in
the specified experiment. No numerical search, discretized estimator
family, or held-out simulation supports the lower bound: it is analytic.
