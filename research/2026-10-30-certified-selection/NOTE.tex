\section{Scope and main contribution}\label{scope-and-main-contribution}

This is an additive contribution to
\texttt{davidiach/adaptive-threshold-estimation}, pinned to commit
\texttt{6ad675e04570cc1ef6bece8e26beb81e1a176471}. It does not rewrite
the baseline, change a frozen specification, or replace a published
manuscript. Its mathematical objects are the selection functional \(K\)
and the closed quantile functional \(Q\) in version 6, Sections 13--14
{[}R1--R3{]}.

The contribution is deterministic. The proofs below do \textbf{not}
assume that the paper-only statistical characterization
\(R_{\mu,\rho}(B)\asymp K_{\mu,\rho}(B)\) has been formalized or
independently validated. They concern the functionals themselves.
Interpreting their numerical values as minimax statistical rates still
relies on the separate statistical theorem and its constants.

\section{Definitions}\label{definitions}

Let \(\mu\) be a probability law on \((0,1/2]\), let \(0<\rho\le1/4\),
let \(B>0\), and set \(h=B^{-1/2}\). Use exactly the repository's
canonical bands

\[
I_j=(2^{-(j+2)},2^{-(j+1)}],\qquad j=0,1,2,\ldots.
\]

For a finite submeasure \(\sigma\le\mu\), write

\[
a_j(\sigma)=\int_{I_j}d^{-2}\,\sigma(dd),\qquad
G(a)=A+\sum_j a_j\log(A/a_j),\qquad A=\sum_j a_j.
\]

Zero coordinates contribute zero, and \(G(0)=0\). For infinitely many
occupied bands, define \(\Gamma_\sigma\) by the supremum of
finite-prefix values of \(G\), as in the repository. Write

\[
c_h(d)=\min\{1,h/d\},\qquad
K_\mu=\min\left\{\rho,\inf_{0\le\sigma\le\mu}
  \left[h\sqrt{\rho\Gamma_\sigma}+\int c_h(d)(\mu-\sigma)(dd)\right]\right\}.
\]

All logarithms are natural. The parameters \(\rho,B\) are suppressed
only when fixed.

For the quantile split, remove exactly the smallest-gap mass \(\rho\):
let \(\nu\) be that lower-tail submeasure and let \(\kappa=\mu-\nu\). At
a boundary atom, split its mass fractionally. There is a positive
quantile location \(t\) such that \(\nu\) is supported on \((0,t]\) and
\(\kappa\) on \([t,1/2]\). Then

\[
T_h=\int c_h\,d\nu,\qquad
Q_\mu=\min\{\rho,T_h+h\sqrt{\rho\Gamma_\kappa}\}.
\]

The core \(\kappa\) meets finitely many bands and has finite
inverse-square cost. No finiteness assumption on the full-law inverse
moment or entropy is needed.

\section{A sharper quantile
comparison}\label{a-sharper-quantile-comparison}

\subsection{Entropy interaction}\label{entropy-interaction}

\textbf{Lemma 1.} For finite nonnegative vectors \(a,b\), with totals
\(A,B\),

\[
G(a+b)\le G(a)+G(b)+2\log 2\sqrt{AB}
          \le G(a)+G(b)+\sqrt{2G(a)G(b)}.
\]

\textbf{Proof.} Expanding the entropy gives

\[
G(a+b)-G(a)-G(b)
\le (A+B)H_2\left(\frac{A}{A+B}\right),
\]

because the omitted coordinatewise terms
\((a_j+b_j)\log(a_j+b_j)-a_j\log a_j-b_j\log b_j\) are nonnegative. Here
\(H_2(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)\).

For completeness,

\[
H_2(p)\le2\log2\sqrt{p(1-p)}.
\]

To see this without appealing to a numerical plot, differentiate
\(H_2(p)/\sqrt{p(1-p)}\) on \((0,1/2)\). Its derivative has the sign of

\[
g(p)=(1-p)\log(1-p)-p\log p.
\]

The function \(g\) is concave on \([0,1/2]\), since
\(g''(p)=-(1-2p)/(p(1-p))\le0\), and vanishes at both endpoints. Thus
the ratio increases to its value \(2\log2\) at \(1/2\); symmetry handles
the other half. Finally, \(A\le G(a)\), \(B\le G(b)\), and
\(2\log2<\sqrt2\). The latter can be verified, for example, using
\(\log2<7/10<1/\sqrt2\). Boundary cases follow by continuity.
\(\square\)

The existing superadditivity property is also important:

\[
G(a+b)\ge G(a)+G(b).
\]

It follows from concavity and degree-one homogeneity. It remains true
for the prefix-supremum extension \(\Gamma\) by taking limits. The upper
interaction bound above will only be used on the finite-band core.

\subsection{A factor-two scale-separated cost
bound}\label{a-factor-two-scale-separated-cost-bound}

\textbf{Lemma 2.} For any finite measure \(\xi\) supported on
\([t,1/2]\),

\[
\Gamma_\xi\le\frac{2}{t}H_\xi,
\qquad H_\xi=\int d^{-1}\,\xi(dd).
\]

\textbf{Proof.} Let \(j_0\) be the band containing \(t\). Index bands
above it by \(r\ge0\), so the band index is \(j_0-r\). Put

\[
m_0=m_1=1,\qquad m_r=2^{r-1}\quad(r\ge2).
\]

Every supported gap in the band at offset \(r\) satisfies
\(d/t\ge m_r\). If \(a_r\) is its inverse-square cost, then

\[
\sum_r m_ra_r\le H_\xi/t.
\]

Use the subprobability weights \(\pi_r=\exp(1-2m_r)\). They satisfy

\[
\sum_r\pi_r
=2e^{-1}+e^{-3}+e^{-7}+e^{-15}+\cdots<1.
\]

One elementary bound is \(2/e<3/4\) and
\(e^{-3}+e^{-7}+\cdots\le e^{-3}/(1-e^{-4})<2/15\). For \(p_r=a_r/A\),
the entropy inequality for a subprobability vector gives
\(H(p)\le\sum_rp_r\log(1/\pi_r)\). Therefore

\[
G(a)\le\sum_r a_r[1+\log(1/\pi_r)]
      =2\sum_r m_ra_r\le2H_\xi/t.
\]

Only finitely many of these bands can be occupied; the unused weights
cause no difficulty. \(\square\)

This improves the factor four used in the pinned comparison proof. The
factor two is not asserted to be optimal.

\subsection{A scalar inequality with an exact algebraic
certificate}\label{a-scalar-inequality-with-an-exact-algebraic-certificate}

\textbf{Lemma 3.} For \(T,v,c\ge0\),

\[
T+\sqrt{v^2+2Tc+2v\sqrt{Tc}}
\le\frac85\left(\sqrt{T^2+v^2}+c\right).
\]

\textbf{Proof.} If \(r=\sqrt{T^2+v^2}=0\), both \(T\) and \(v\) vanish
and the result is immediate. Otherwise divide by \(r\), put
\(s=T/r\in[0,1]\), and write \(z=\sqrt{c/r}\). It suffices to show

\[
s+\sqrt{1-s^2+2sz^2+2z\sqrt{s(1-s^2)}}
\le\frac85(1+z^2).
\]

The right side minus \(s\) is positive. On squaring, the difference
between its square and the radicand is

\[
P=\frac{64}{25}z^4+
 \left(\frac{128}{25}-\frac{26}{5}s\right)z^2
 -2z\sqrt{s(1-s^2)}+2s^2-\frac{16}{5}s+\frac{39}{25}.
\]

Young's inequality gives

\[
2z\sqrt{s(1-s^2)}\le\frac32z^2+\frac23s(1-s^2).
\]

Consequently,

\[
P\ge\frac{64}{25}
 \left(z^2+\frac{181-260s}{256}\right)^2+\frac{p(s)}{3072},
\]

where

\[
p(s)=2048s^3-1968s^2-584s+861.
\]

This is an exact polynomial identity, not a fitted numerical inequality.
With \(w=s-3/4\ge-3/4\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
p(s)&=2048w^3+2640w^2-80w+180\\
&\ge1104w^2-80w+180\\
&=1104\left(w-\frac{5}{138}\right)^2+\frac{12320}{69}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The required squared inequality follows, and then so does the unsquared
inequality. \(\square\)

\subsection{Main theorem}\label{main-theorem}

\textbf{Theorem 4.} For every law and parameters in Section 2,

\[
\boxed{K_\mu\le Q_\mu\le\frac85K_\mu.}
\]

Also \(T_h\le K_\mu\). If \(h\ge t\), then \(K_\mu=Q_\mu=\rho\).

\textbf{Proof.} The inequality \(K\le Q\) follows by choosing
\(\sigma=\kappa\). Fix any finite-objective selection \(\sigma=f\mu\),
where \(0\le f\le1\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
u&=h\sqrt{\rho\Gamma_{f\nu}},&
v&=h\sqrt{\rho\Gamma_{f\kappa}},\\
a&=\int c_h(d)(1-f)\,\nu(dd),&
c&=\int c_h(d)(1-f)\,\kappa(dd).
\end{aligned}
\]

In this display, the scalar \(u\) is distinct from the measure \(\nu\).
Write \(x=h\sqrt{\rho\Gamma_\sigma}\) and \(S=x+a+c\) for the selection
objective. Because \(\sigma=f\nu+f\kappa\), superadditivity implies

\[
u^2+v^2\le x^2.
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz and \(\nu((0,1/2])=\rho\) give

\[
T_h\le a+h\int d^{-1}f\,d\nu
\le a+h\sqrt{\rho\int d^{-2}f\,d\nu}
\le a+u.
\]

In particular \(T_h\le S\) for every selection, and \(T_h\le\rho\),
proving \(T_h\le K\). If \(h\ge t\), then \(T_h=\rho\) and the
saturation claim follows.

Assume now \(h<t\). Put \(\xi=(1-f)\kappa\) and
\(z_0=h\sqrt{\rho\Gamma_\xi}\). Since the core gaps exceed \(h\), we
have \(c=hH_\xi\). Also every tail gap is at most \(t\), giving
\(h\rho/t\le T_h\). Lemma 2 therefore yields

\[
z_0^2\le2(h\rho/t)(hH_\xi)\le2T_hc.
\]

Apply Lemma 1 to \(\kappa=f\kappa+\xi\):

\[
h\sqrt{\rho\Gamma_\kappa}
\le\sqrt{v^2+z_0^2+\sqrt2\,vz_0}
\le\sqrt{v^2+2T_hc+2v\sqrt{T_hc}}.
\]

Finally, the Euclidean triangle inequality gives

\[
\sqrt{T_h^2+v^2}
\le\sqrt{(u+a)^2+v^2}
\le\sqrt{u^2+v^2}+a\le x+a.
\]

Lemma 3 now shows

\[
T_h+h\sqrt{\rho\Gamma_\kappa}
\le\frac85\left(\sqrt{T_h^2+v^2}+c\right)
\le\frac85S.
\]

Take the infimum over \(\sigma\) and retain the outer cap at \(\rho\).
This proves the theorem. All infinite-law steps concern either monotone
extended entropies or the finite-band core; no exchange of an infimum
with an infinite limit is needed here. \(\square\)

\textbf{Why the proof is stronger.} The earlier comparison uses the
total selected entropy both to control the selected tail and to control
the selected core. Here the two costs remain separate until the
inequality \(u^2+v^2\le x^2\) combines them. That avoids charging the
same selected cost twice.

\section{Exact selection within
bands}\label{exact-selection-within-bands}

\textbf{Theorem 5 (bandwise suffix reduction).} Let \(\mu\) be a finite
law on \(n\) distinct positive gaps. If band \(j\) contains \(n_j\)
atoms, a globally minimizing hard selection exists that, within every
occupied band, selects a suffix of the atoms in increasing gap order.
Thus an exact search need examine only

\[
\prod_j(n_j+1)
\]

assignments, rather than all \(2^n\) hard subsets.

\textbf{Proof.} Allow fractional selections \(x_i\in[0,1]\). The
finite-dimensional objective is continuous on a compact cube, so it
attains a minimum. Fix the selections outside one band. In that band put

\[
\alpha_i=w_i/d_i^2,\qquad \beta_i=w_ic_h(d_i).
\]

Its contribution to the entropy depends on the selected atoms only
through \(z=\sum_i\alpha_ix_i\). At fixed \(z\), minimizing the
objective is equivalent to maximizing the saved ordinary cost
\(\sum_i\beta_ix_i\). The benefit-to-cost ratio is

\[
\frac{\beta_i}{\alpha_i}=d_i^2c_h(d_i)
=\begin{cases}d_i^2,&d_i\le h,\\hd_i,&d_i\ge h.\end{cases}
\]

This is strictly increasing in the gap. The fractional-knapsack exchange
argument therefore selects the largest gaps first, with at most one
fractional boundary atom. Explicitly, moving a small amount of selected
inverse-square cost from a smaller gap to a larger, not-yet-full gap
preserves \(z\) and increases the saved ordinary cost.

Between consecutive cumulative suffix costs, the maximized saving is
affine in \(z\). The entropy contribution, with the other bands fixed,
is concave in \(z\): in the positive interior its second derivative is
\(1/(A_0+z)-1/z\le0\). Its square root is also concave, since square
root is increasing and concave. Thus on each such interval the complete
objective is concave, and its minimum occurs at an endpoint. The
fractional boundary atom may be removed or included in full without
increasing the minimum.

Perform this replacement band by band. Each replacement preserves global
optimality, leaves the already processed bands as suffixes, and makes
the new band a hard suffix. This proves the claim. The outer cap by
\(\rho\) does not affect the argument. \(\square\)

This theorem does \textbf{not} give a polynomial-time algorithm in the
unrestricted number of bands. When there is one atom per band, the
candidate count is still \(2^n\). It can nevertheless make a large
difference when many atoms occupy the same few bands. For 60 atoms in
one band, the sufficient family contains 61 candidates instead of
\(2^{60}\); this is a reduction against naive subset enumeration, not a
claim that every previous implementation required that enumeration.

\subsection{A cheaper global-cut
approximation}\label{a-cheaper-global-cut-approximation}

Let \(V\) be the minimum of the capped objective over the \(n+1\)
selections that keep all atoms above a single global gap threshold.

\textbf{Corollary 6.} \(K\le V\le Q\le\tfrac85K\).

\textbf{Proof.} The quantile core is a global cut with at most one
fractional boundary atom. Its objective is concave in that atom's
selected fraction, with every other atom fixed. At least one of the two
endpoints is no worse than the fractional value. Both endpoints are
among the global cuts. \(\square\)

The implementation precomputes ordinary-cost prefixes and updates the
band-cost vector as the cut moves. It evaluates \(n+1\) candidates, each
involving at most the number of occupied bands. This is a certified
feasible upper bound, \textbf{not} an exact optimizer in general.

For example, with masses \(9999/10000\) at \(1/4\) and \(1/10000\) at
\(1/2\), rarity \(1/4\), and budget \(10^8\), the optimum selects the
smaller-gap atom and rejects the larger-gap atom. Its value is
approximately \(0.0002000099997499875\), while the best global upper-gap
cut is approximately \(0.0002000214902134999\). The distinction is small
in this example but exact. The certificate is
\texttt{evidence/not\_one\_global\_threshold.json}.

\section{Stability and controlled approximation of a
law}\label{stability-and-controlled-approximation-of-a-law}

\subsection{Total variation}\label{total-variation}

Use the probability-measure convention

\[
d_{\rm TV}(\mu,\widetilde\mu)
=\sup_A|\mu(A)-\widetilde\mu(A)|
=\tfrac12\|\mu-\widetilde\mu\|_1.
\]

\textbf{Theorem 7.} At fixed \(\rho,B\),

\[
\boxed{|K_\mu-K_{\widetilde\mu}|\le d_{\rm TV}(\mu,\widetilde\mu).}
\]

\textbf{Proof.} Let \(\lambda=\mu\wedge\widetilde\mu\) be the common
submeasure and let \(\eta=1-\lambda((0,1/2])\). For any
\(\sigma\le\mu\), set \(\sigma_0=\sigma\wedge\lambda\). Then
\(\sigma_0\le\widetilde\mu\) and \(\Gamma_{\sigma_0}\le\Gamma_\sigma\).
Moreover,

\[
\widetilde\mu-\sigma_0
=(\widetilde\mu-\lambda)+(\lambda-\sigma_0),
\qquad \lambda-\sigma_0\le\mu-\sigma.
\]

Since \(0\le c_h\le1\), the remainder cost increases by at most
\(\eta\). Taking infima and clipping at \(\rho\) gives
\(K_{\widetilde\mu}\le K_\mu+\eta\). Interchange the laws. \(\square\)

The unit constant cannot be improved uniformly. Fix \(0<\eta<\rho\);
start with a point mass at a fixed easy gap and move mass \(\eta\) to a
gap \(d_h=o(h)\) as \(h\downarrow0\). The original score tends to zero,
while the perturbed score is at least \(\eta\) by the tail lower bound
and at most \(\eta+O(h)\) by assigning the hard mass to the remainder.

This is stability of the \textbf{optimal rate functional}, not an
assertion that a fixed estimator designed for one law continues to meet
its budget or risk guarantee under another law. In particular, no
guarantee about estimating \(\mu\) from pilot data is being made. An
empirical atomic measure and a continuous law have total variation
distance one; replacing a continuous law by its empirical distribution
does not automatically make this theorem useful.

\subsection{Band-preserving upward
rounding}\label{band-preserving-upward-rounding}

\textbf{Theorem 8.} Let \(T:(0,1/2]\to(0,1/2]\) be measurable, preserve
every canonical band, and satisfy \(d\le T(d)\le rd\) for some
\(r\ge1\). For \(\widetilde\mu=T_\#\mu\),

\[
\boxed{K_{\widetilde\mu}\le K_\mu\le rK_{\widetilde\mu}.}
\]

\textbf{Proof.} Push any \(\sigma\le\mu\) forward. Since each gap stays
in its band,

\[
r^{-2}a_j(\sigma)\le a_j(T_\#\sigma)\le a_j(\sigma).
\]

Coordinatewise monotonicity and homogeneity give the corresponding
entropy inequalities, while \(c_h(T(d))\le c_h(d)\). This proves
\(K_{\widetilde\mu}\le K_\mu\).

Conversely, write any \(\widetilde\sigma\le\widetilde\mu\) as
\(f\widetilde\mu\) and lift it to \(\sigma=(f\circ T)\mu\). Its
pushforward is \(\widetilde\sigma\) even when \(T\) merges atoms. Then
\(\Gamma_\sigma\le r^2\Gamma_{\widetilde\sigma}\) and
\(c_h(d)\le r c_h(T(d))\). The lifted objective is at most \(r\) times
the rounded objective. Take infima and clip. \(\square\)

\textbf{The band condition is necessary for this argument.} Consider
equal masses at \(249/1000\) and \(251/1000\), with rarity \(1/100\) and
budget \(10^8\). Rounding the first gap upward to the second changes it
by a factor of only \(251/249\approx1.00803\), but merges two dyadic
bands. Certified scores are approximately

\[
K_{\rm before}=5.204915296814786\cdot10^{-5},\qquad
K_{\rm after}=3.984063745019918\cdot10^{-5}.
\]

Their ratio is about \(1.3064\), not at most \(1.00803\). This is a
feature of the dyadic entropy functional; it is not a demonstrated
discontinuity of the underlying statistical minimax risk. The
implementation's rounding helper checks band preservation exactly.

\subsection{A finite approximation theorem for arbitrary
laws}\label{a-finite-approximation-theorem-for-arbitrary-laws}

\textbf{Corollary 9.} Suppose \(d_{\rm TV}(\mu,\mu_0)\le\eta\), and a
band-preserving upward map with distortion at most \(r\) sends \(\mu_0\)
to a finite law \(\widehat\mu\). If a numerical certificate encloses
\(K_{\widehat\mu}\) in \([L,U]\), then

\[
\boxed{\max\{0,L-\eta\}\le K_\mu
\le\min\{\rho,rU+\eta\}.}
\]

In particular, evaluating the finite quantile functional gives

\[
\max\{0,\tfrac58Q_{\widehat\mu}-\eta\}
\le K_\mu\le\min\{\rho,rQ_{\widehat\mu}+\eta\}.
\]

\textbf{Proof.} Compose Theorems 7 and 8, then apply Theorem 4 when
using \(Q\). \(\square\)

Every law on \((0,1/2]\) admits such finite approximations with
\(\eta\downarrow0\) and \(r\downarrow1\). Choose \(t>0\) so that
\(\mu((0,t))\le\eta\), move that small tail mass to \(t\), and round the
remaining support upward on a finite grid inside each of its finitely
many bands. With \(m\) equally spaced subintervals per band, the
relative distortion is at most \(1+1/m\).

This is an approximation theorem, not a universal time bound: finding
\(t\) and the exact cell masses requires suitable access to the law, and
the required number of bands can be large. Certified enclosures for
input masses would require an additional layer beyond the exact-rational
law API implemented here.

\section{The numerical implementation and its trust
boundary}\label{the-numerical-implementation-and-its-trust-boundary}

The new module is
\texttt{toolkit/threshold\_toolkit/rare\_event\_functional.py}. It uses
only the Python standard library. It never uses binary floating-point
arithmetic to form a bound. Decimal displays in the experiment summaries
are explicitly separate from the rational endpoints in the certificates.

\subsection{Rational arithmetic
primitives}\label{rational-arithmetic-primitives}

For \(x=n/d\ge0\) and precision \(p\), let

\[
k=\left\lfloor\sqrt{\left\lfloor n2^{2p}/d\right\rfloor}\right\rfloor.
\]

Then \(k/2^p\le\sqrt{x}<(k+1)/2^p\), unless the lower endpoint is exact,
in which case the interval collapses. The integer square root supplies
these bounds without an approximate transcendental function.

For a positive rational \(x\), reduce it to \(x=2^ky\) with
\(1\le y<2\). With \(z=(y-1)/(y+1)\in[0,1/3)\),

\[
\log y=2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_N,
\quad
0\le R_N\le\frac{2z^{2N+1}}{(2N+1)(1-z^2)}.
\]

The same formula at \(z=1/3\) bounds \(\log2\). This gives rational
enclosures for the entropy. Monotonicity then gives rational bounds for
each complete objective. Taking the minimum of all candidate lower
endpoints and the minimum of all candidate upper endpoints encloses the
global minimum whenever the sufficient candidate family has been
completely enumerated.

A best feasible witness is not automatically declared the unique
optimizer when its interval overlaps another candidate's interval. A
search exceeding the explicit state cap raises an exception; it never
emits an incomplete search as an exact certificate.

\subsection{Public entry points}\label{public-entry-points}

\texttt{quantile\_certificate} computes an inexpensive lower/upper
enclosure using \(T\le K\), \(5Q/8\le K\le Q\), and the ordinary-only
and full-entropy feasible selections.

\texttt{threshold\_certificate} scans all global upper-gap cuts for a
potentially tighter feasible upper bound. Its output explicitly records
that it is not globally exact.

\texttt{exhaustive\_certificate} uses the bandwise-suffix theorem and
encloses the exact finite-law functional. It precomputes each band's
suffix cost and complementary ordinary cost, so a candidate evaluation
visits bands rather than all atoms.

\texttt{round\_up\_within\_bands} constructs a valid finite-law rounding
map and returns its exact maximum relative distortion.
\texttt{transfer\_enclosure} applies Corollary 9; it does not infer or
validate a total-variation premise from samples.

\texttt{verify\_certificate} takes the expected law, rarity, and budget
separately. It checks the schema and scope, binds the instance by
canonical rational values and a SHA-256 digest, reconstructs the
required calculation, and compares the complete canonical output. It
rejects extra, missing, or corrupted fields. This is deterministic
recomputation, \textbf{not} an independent implementation or a formal
proof of the Python code.

\section{Validation actually
performed}\label{validation-actually-performed}

The final unit suite contains \textbf{29 tests}, all passing. A separate
standard-library checker verifies all four polynomial identities in
Lemma 3 coefficient by coefficient over the rationals; its record is
\texttt{evidence/algebra.json}. It covers exact dyadic boundaries,
positive gaps as small as \(2^{-600}\), invalid inputs, exact
single-atom formulas, quantile atom splitting, saturation, monotonicity,
same-band search reduction, independent full-subset comparisons,
total-variation and rounding examples, the exact scalar polynomial
identity, certificate round trips, and deliberate corruption of scope,
instance, endpoints, completion status, and other metadata.

A separate deterministic run examined \textbf{128 rational laws},
comparing \textbf{9,604 full hard subsets} against \textbf{5,211
band-suffix candidates}. The interval results were consistent in every
case. The quantile bounds also enclosed the independently enumerated
optima. These are finite regression checks, not proofs of the universal
theorems.

An additional floating-point exploration examined \textbf{20,000 laws}
and \textbf{1,439,604 subsets}. Its largest observed ratio was
approximately 1.4213200455. A nearby rationalized example was then
certified separately, yielding the strict \(1.42132\) lower bound in
Section 6. The floating-point exploration itself has no certificate
status and was not used to justify the \(8/5\) theorem.

All these runs use newly generated cases. No repository held-out
benchmark data, sealed research inputs, or existing result files were
changed. The complete upstream Lean suite and the complete pre-existing
toolkit suite were \textbf{not} rerun. No claim is made about a new
GitHub CI run. The container had no Lean executable, and a full network
clone failed; the relevant upstream sources were read through the GitHub
connector.

\section{Integration, remaining work, and related
work}\label{integration-remaining-work-and-related-work}

\subsection{Integration boundary}\label{integration-boundary}

The package is additive: one module, one test file, this research note,
and its reproducibility files. It does not alter the frozen entropy-tail
specification, published papers, or existing audit records. The
mathematical proofs here are not Lean proofs. The natural formalization
targets are, in dependency order: the entropy interaction bound; the
scale-separated bound; the scalar inequality; the \(8/5\) comparison;
the finite-band suffix reduction; and the two approximation
inequalities.

The scalar inequality is particularly suitable for a small isolated
algebraic proof. The bandwise result requires a finite-dimensional
exchange argument, not the statistical experiment model. None of these
targets should be recorded as verified merely because the Python tests
pass.

\subsection{What this does not
resolve}\label{what-this-does-not-resolve}

The full general-law entropy-tail statistical lower and upper bounds
remain outside this contribution's formalization scope. This package
does not produce a practical coin-flipping implementation of the paper's
general rare-event estimator, establish a sharp high-confidence
rare-event law, prove unknown-law adaptation, convert expected budgets
into deterministic budgets, or eliminate the large constants in the
statistical theorem. Its narrower improvement is a much tighter and
auditable computational interface to the existing rate functional.

The absence of external specialist review remains a limitation, not an
added completion gate. The repository's documented project preference is
preserved.

\subsection{Related-work and novelty
boundary}\label{related-work-and-novelty-boundary}

The repository already contains the entropy-tail selection law, the
closed quantile law, and the hard-selection lemma {[}R1--R3{]}. These
are not reintroduced here as new discoveries. Lee and Valiant study
fully adaptive estimation of mixtures of unknown positive and negative
coins, including the rare-event dependence on error, gap, and confidence
{[}R4{]}. The September 2026 papers by Aronow, Kallus, and Lopatto
{[}R5{]}, and by Yao, Zhao, and Zhou {[}R6{]}, concern Gaussian best-arm
identification and complexity-weighted gap entropy. They are relevant
antecedents, but they are different statistical tasks.

In particular, the appearance of an additive confidence/entropy term in
best-arm identification does not by itself prove the analogous formula
for the present rare-event model. No such transfer is claimed. This
contribution makes no assertion that its deterministic inequalities or
finite optimization structure are unprecedented outside the repository;
a complete priority survey has not been performed.
