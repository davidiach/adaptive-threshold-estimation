\begin{abstract}
We investigate acquisition feedback as a possible escape from the verified
single-coin and additive obstructions. In a two-gap experiment with a
deterministic cap of two flips, an explicit feedback policy has smaller
minimax squared error over the full global-rarity class than every
preselected independent acquisition design, including arbitrary nonlinear
postprocessing. Exact rational inequalities certify the separation.
Conversely, polynomial moment matching gives two globally rare quadratic-law
populations that are indistinguishable to every feedback algorithm with a
fixed local observation cap. The same construction imposes a quantitative
depth-tail requirement on unrestricted algorithms. A separate approximation
theorem reduces the unrestricted expected-budget value to finite convex
programs with explicit risk and budget errors for arbitrary known laws.
A finite-prior greedy
feedback estimator was implemented and evaluated in a frozen synthetic
suite of7,680 runs. Its point estimates are encouraging, but establish
neither a uniform error guarantee nor stable practical superiority.
The arbitrary-law expected-budget minimax problem remains unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Evidence and scope.}
The mathematical statements are \textbf{PROVED-ON-PAPER} with separate
adversarial agent review and explicitly limited exact arithmetic checks.
They are not Lean formalized or externally peer reviewed. The simulation
is \textbf{NUMERICAL}, in float64 with recorded seeds. No novelty or priority
claim is made. A deterministic two-flip cap must not be replaced by an
expected-work budget of two; an algorithm can satisfy the latter while
occasionally taking more observations.

\section{Experiment and unresolved objective}
A fresh iid coin has hidden $D\sim\mu$ on $(0,1/2]$ and
$S\mid D=d\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q(d))$. Its conditional flips are iid
Bernoulli with mean $1/2+(2S-1)D$. The unknown measurable allocation satisfies
$0\le q\le1$ and the global constraint $\theta(q):=\int q\,d\mu\le\rho$.
Known $\mu,\rho$ do not reveal any sampled magnitude or sign. The algorithm
sees only coin identities and bits and can create, revisit, interleave,
abandon and stop adaptively. Genuine flips, including pilots, cost one each;
deterministic computation and independent randomization are free in the
statistical model. Actual computation time is accounted for separately.

The primary risk is minimax RMS under $\sup_q\E_qT\le B$. The candidate
arbitrary-law formula is
\[
 \Psi_{\mu,\rho}(B^{-1/2}),\qquad
 \Psi_{\mu,\rho}(h)=\sup_{0\le w\le1,\int w\,d\mu\le\rho}
 \int\min\{w(d),h\sqrt{w(d)}/d\}\,\mu(dd).
\]
For the exact quadratic law on $(0,1/4]$, the candidate unsaturated order
is $\sqrt{\rho L/B}$, $L=1+\log(1/\rho)$. The inherited single-coin and
specified additive classes lose $\sqrt{1+\log L}$. This pass asks whether
genuine acquisition feedback can avoid that obstruction, without assuming
that it does.

\section{A concrete nonlinear feedback estimator}
For a specified finite law, choose a finite prior on feasible allocations
using only known $\mu,\rho$. The prior includes the null, masses $\rho/2$
and $\rho$, and uniform, hardest-first, easiest-first, inverse-square and
feasible single-atom profiles. The true allocation is not supplied to the
policy. Conditional on each prior hypothesis, coin likelihoods factor over
their observed local words. Thus an existing coin's next-bit probability
can be computed from its heads/tails counts, and the posterior weights
on allocations update after every actual bit.

For action $a$ (a fresh or existing coin), let $p_a(q)$ be that next-bit
probability. Its one-step posterior variance reduction is exactly
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(\theta,p_a)^2}
      {\overline p_a(1-\overline p_a)},\qquad
 \overline p_a=\E[p_a\mid\text{observed transcript}].
\]
Select the action maximizing this quantity, with a deterministic tie rule,
and return the final posterior mean after exactly $B$ flips. This is an
implementable nonlinear feedback heuristic. One-step optimality under a
finite prior is neither global Bayes optimality nor a guarantee over
arbitrary $q$. No claim of calibrated uncertainty under misspecification
is made.

The frozen suite uses two- and four-gap laws, $\rho=1/4,1/16$, five
allocations including an out-of-prior mixture, budgets64,256,1024,
and32 replicates per cell. Comparators use the same posterior estimator
with fresh-only or fixed blocks of4 or16 flips, plus zero, half and rarity
midpoint constants. All sampling procedures use the identical observation
interface and exactly $B$ charged flips. The total evaluation used
3,440,640 genuine flips in41.22seconds. The source, configurations and seeds
were locked before evaluation; no post-result tuning was performed.

Feedback had a smaller MSE point estimate than the best fixed acquisition
comparator in40 of60 cells, and a smaller maximum tested MSE in all12
law/rarity/budget groups. At $B=1024$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
Law & $\rho$ & Feedback max MSE & Best fixed max MSE\\\hline
Two gaps & 1/4 & 0.0034615 & 0.0044203\\
Two gaps & 1/16 & 0.0005496 & 0.0013175\\
Four gaps & 1/4 & 0.0022861 & 0.0054182\\
Four gaps & 1/16 & 0.0004891 & 0.0012849
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These are maxima over only five tested profiles and noisy finite-replicate
estimates, not minimax certificates. Complete bias, variance, raw/clipped
RMS, target-miss probability and bounded-loss uncertainty are in the data
tables. The uncertainty bounds are wide and cellwise, not simultaneous.
No confidence-interval estimator or coverage result is supplied. Outputs
already lie in $[0,\rho]$, so clipping to $[0,1]$ changes nothing.

Independent replay of one predesignated replicate in every one of240 cells
matched outputs and acquisition counts. An independent code audit found no
access to actual $q,D,S$ and checked every stored work count. Row timings
share deterministic prediction caches and omit model setup; the overall
timer includes setup. There are zero extra pilot observations, but setup
has nonzero computational cost. No one-shot speedup or amortized break-even
claim follows from these timings.

\section{Remaining proof obligations and source positioning}
A successful pilot-selected production rule needs a response-weighted
calibration guarantee. Conditional on a pilot, let $g=m_+-m_-$ denote the
production response and let it be calibrated at a fitted $\widehat q$.
Its actual bias is
\[
 \int(q-\widehat q)(g-1)\,d\mu.
\]
Matching a few pilot moments or an estimated target does not bound this
quantity. The local-depth theorem supplies an exact failure of shallow
identification, while deeper production remains a possible escape.
Independent pilot and production data do not remove conditional bias.

Lee--Valiant's Section7 oracle construction optimizes stopping flows and
terminal coefficients for known conditional populations and a specified
cap. Learning those populations with a uniform robust calibration guarantee
is an additional obligation here. Juditsky--Nemirovski's product-experiment
comparison is relevant to fixed acquisition; a concurrent repository task
is developing its faithful transcript transfer and is not adopted as a
completed dependency. The focused primary-source audit accompanies this
note. Classical moment matching, score identities and convex comparison
arguments are not claimed original.

The exact feedback gain is a finite cap-two effect; the small-rarity
information calculation does not amplify it into the missing logarithm.
The depth-tail hard pair changes with $K$, so those tail bounds cannot be
summed as though they held at one fixed population. The finite-prior
heuristic has no uniform-q guarantee. These limitations leave the original
arbitrary-law expected-budget rate and confidence dependence open.
