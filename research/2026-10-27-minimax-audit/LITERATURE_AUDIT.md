### Literal class-membership and minimax-quantifier check (new audit reasoning)

This is a **PROVED-ON-PAPER verification of an inherited construction**, not a new lower-bound theorem or Lean proof.

Fix `a0=1/4` once. Let `C` be the union over **all finite partitions and all finite sign arrays** constructed as follows: the outer state is uniform on `[0,1]`; a disclosed, cellwise linear map `D` has exactly uniform law on `(0,a0]`; each cell has one unknown fixed sign; and a query at `u` returns `X=2 Bernoulli(1/2+s D(u))-1`. Here `s` is ±1. This definition makes `C` fixed before choosing an accuracy. It does not impose a fixed number of cells or a uniform bound on the slope of `D`.

For every model in `C`, `Var(X|u)=1-4D(u)^2 >=3/4` and `|X-E[X|u]|<=3/2`. Thus its standardized conditional kth absolute moment is at most `3^(k/2)` for every finite `k`; the bound is independent of partition size, accuracy, and sign array. If `Z=2D/sqrt(1-4D²)`, the inverse map is `D=Z/(2 sqrt(1+Z²))`, so the density of `Z` is `1/(2 a0 (1+Z²)^(3/2))` on `(0,1/sqrt(3)]`, zero outside. Its essential bound is **2**, again uniform. Versions at finitely many endpoints do not change the law; define them positively as in v4.

At each accuracy, v4 chooses a finite number of dyadic bands and finitely many cells per band. Every resulting deterministic model lies in this same `C`. The valid lower-bound quantifiers are

`exists C,c,eps0 >0; for all eps<eps0; for every A valid uniformly on C; sup_{M in C} E_M T_A >= c eps^-2 log²(1/eps)`.

The construction permits the hard member/finite prior to depend on `eps`. It does **not** claim one fixed finite cell array is asymptotically hard. This distinction is essential because sufficiently sampling every member of a fixed finite collection is a different experiment.

The lower experiment even allows arbitrary selected-state queries: observations within one cell may have varying magnitudes but share its single sign, so sign-conditional likelihood factors multiply, and `D(u) eta_cell<=h/8` gives the same one-step posterior KL bound. The upper algorithm can ignore the state labels and use independent uniform states. At most one new state per inner observation is needed, so charging a fixed outer-draw cost `c_out` changes that upper work by at most a factor `1+c_out`; an inner-work lower bound still lower-bounds total work. An unbounded or model-dependent outer cost is not covered by this argument.

This comparison is exact for GHA's stated density/moment assumptions. It settles the rate within an **unstructured** subclass, with constants fixed independently of accuracy. It says nothing about the minimax rate of a single known parametric simulator, a rare-sign restriction, or a class with bounded cross-state smoothness. Its RMS version uses Markov at radius `4 eps` and failure `1/16`, and changes constants only. Expected work cannot be replaced by a hard cap in the upper theorem.

### Giles's discontinuous-MLMC survey

[arXiv:2301.02882v1, 7 January 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.02882v1), §§1 and 7 inspected. **OVERLAPPING**. The survey explains adaptive refinement, the `C_l V_l` complexity criterion, and high kurtosis of rare nonzero corrections. ## 5. What the existing squared-log lower bound contributes

Subject to its paper proof and finite-to-unbounded transcript dependencies, it supplies an all-algorithm expected-work obstruction for a fixed **unstructured observed-state class** meeting GHA's explicit assumptions uniformly. The upper exponent and its logarithm were already established in adaptive MLMC. The lower bound's potential contribution is the all-algorithm statement, including selected-state access, together with a hard class that actually lies inside the cited assumption set.

This audit found no explicit match to that exact statement in the inspected passages. That is not a priority finding. It neither establishes novelty nor answers lower-bound questions for smoother regression, analytic simulators, known conditional laws, or the rare-event class. A human specialist's assessment remains pending.
