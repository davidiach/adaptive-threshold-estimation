## Minimax law and unbiasedness

[Theorem 1 in §2](../paper/paper.tex#L239) defines the minimax RMS risk

$$R_\mu(B)=\inf_{\mathcal A:\,\sup_q\mathbb E_{\mu,q}T\le B}\ \sup_q\bigl(\mathbb E_{\mu,q}(\widehat\theta-\theta)^2\bigr)^{1/2}.$$

For every probability law $\mu$ on $(0,1/2]$, $B\ge1$, and $0<\delta\le1/4$, it proves

$$R_\mu(B)\asymp\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2}),\qquad r_\mu(B,\delta)\asymp\omega_\mu\!\left(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/B}\right),\qquad \omega_\mu(h)=\mathbb E_\mu\min\{1,h/D\}.$$

Here $r_\mu(B,\delta)$ is the minimax uniform error radius at failure probability at most $\delta$, with the same expected-budget constraint. The constants are universal. The algorithms depend only on $B$, or on $(B,\delta)$, and observe neither magnitudes nor their law. The proof is in §5. Both the RMS and confidence characterizations now have Lean proofs in both directions in the original observable adaptive model.

The lower constant is settled for $B\ge1$. The upper constant is not, so the two sides remain a factor $72$ apart.

**The upper constants 119, 45, 36, 27, 19, 17, 15, 13 and 12.** The constants $512$,
$119$, $45$ and $36$ come from the same distribution-blind estimator, which
knows only the budget; $27$, $19$, $17$, $15$ and $13$ randomize between two of its
levels, and $12$ runs one level at a calibration scale that need not be a
power of two. Its analysis
bounds the second moment and the cost of one call by level sums. The library
proves those sums below $23$ and $527/7$, but the proofs of the frozen Tier 2
targets weaken them to $85$ and $149$. Kept sharp, they give a mean-square
error of at most $12289/7$ times the squared modulus at the dyadic budget
level. Comparing the level with the budget by the factor $17/6$ in place of
$4$ then gives $119$ ([record](../results/upper-constant-20261118/README.md)).

The constant $45$ also sharpens the expected number of flips of each
early-stopping classifier. The cap-$N$ classifier passes its check at prefix
$n$ only if the running mean stays within
$t_n=\sqrt{(1+\log(N/n))/n}$ of one half, so Hoeffding's inequality bounds
that probability by $\exp(-2n(d-t_n)^2)$ once $t_n\le d$. Summing the dyadic
terms with their offset gives at most
$\tfrac{49}{10}\,d^{-2}(1+\log(Nd^2))$ flips when $Nd^2\ge1$, in place of
$17$ times the work envelope. Summing over levels, with each level whose
cap satisfies $Nd^2\le32$ bounded by that cap, bounds the call cost by
$248/25$ in place of $527/7$. The
mean-square error is then at most $6304/25$ times the squared modulus, and
$\sqrt{6304/25}\cdot17/6<45$
([record](../results/upper-work-20261118/README.md)).

The constant $36$ also sharpens the call second moment. Two consecutive
classifiers disagree only if one of them errs, and each errs with probability
at most $\tfrac53e^{-2}/(Nd^2)^2$, so they disagree with probability at most
$\tfrac{25}{3}e^{-2}/(Nd^2)^2\le\tfrac{113}{100}/(Nd^2)^2$; the frozen Tier 2
proof rounds the factor up to $3$. Summing over levels, with the levels whose
cap satisfies $Nd^2\le1$ bounded by $1$, bounds the call second moment by
$72/5$ in place of $23$. The mean-square error is then at most $19781/125$
times the squared modulus, and $\sqrt{19781/125}\cdot17/6<36$
([record](../results/upper-moment-20261118/README.md)).

The constant $27$ removes the loss of the dyadic budget level. For
$4\cdot2^L\le B<8\cdot2^L$ the estimator above runs level $L$, which costs at
most $4\cdot2^L$, and can leave up to half the budget unused; the factor
$17/6$ pays for that. The mixture runs level $L+1$ with probability
$p=B/(4\cdot2^L)-1\in[0,1]$ and level $L$ otherwise, chosen by a coin in its
random seed, so its expected work is at most $B$ under every sign law. Its
mean-square error is the same mixture of the two levels' errors. Since
$N\,\omega_\mu(N^{-1/2})^2\le B\,\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2})^2$ for $0<N\le B$, it is at
most $4(1+p)(1-p/2)\le9/2$ times $19781/125$ times
$\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2})^2$, and $\sqrt{(9/2)\cdot19781/125}<27$
([record](../results/upper-mixture-20261118/README.md)). The mixture is still
chosen before the gap law and knows only the budget.

The constant $19$ sharpens the cost of one call of the same mixture. The
check with $n$ flips of the cap-$N$ classifier survives with probability at
most $\exp(-2n(d-t_n)^2)$ once its threshold $t_n$ is below $d$. The constant
$45$ bounded $t_n$ by $\sqrt{u/n}$ with $u=1+\log(Nd^2)$ and dropped $u$ from
the exponent. Summed over the pairs of a level $l$ and a check $k<l$, the
threshold depends only on $j=l-k$: it is below $d$ exactly when
$2^kd^2\ge v_j=1+j\log2$. So the pairs with a fixed $j$ follow one dyadic grid
of $2^kd^2/v_j$, and offset bounds on those grids, $89/20$ for $j=1$ and $17/4$
for $j\ge2$, bound the padded call cost by $\tfrac{11}{2}\min(\sqrt N,1/d)$ in
place of $273/25$. The geometric extension of each grid below $k=0$ also pays
for the one flip that the padded call takes above level $L$. The batch
mean-square error is then at most $401/5=1+\tfrac{72}{5}\cdot\tfrac{11}{2}$
times the squared modulus, and $\sqrt{(9/2)\cdot401/5}<19$
([record](../results/upper-cost-20261118/README.md)).

The constant $17$ sharpens the call second moment of the same mixture. Two
consecutive classifiers disagree only if one of them errs, and the cap-$N$
classifier errs with probability at most its union bound: $e^{-2Nd^2}$ at the
cap plus $\exp(-2n(d+t_n)^2)$ for each early check. The constant $36$ rounded
that bound to $\tfrac53e^{-2}/(Nd^2)^2$. At level $l$, with $x=2^ld^2$, the two
cap terms are $e^{-2x}$ and $e^{-x}$. Along the dyadic grid of $x$, with the
levels below $x=12/25$ bounded by one, their weighted sum is at most
$321/100$. The early-stopping terms keep the cross term of $(d+t_n)^2$, and a
telescoping bound adds at most $41/100$ for them. So $d$ times the call second
moment is at most $(2+\sqrt2)\cdot\tfrac{321}{100}+\tfrac{41}{100}\le\tfrac{57}{5}$.
When $2^Ld^2\le1$, bounding every level by one gives $(6+4\sqrt2)\sqrt N$
instead, above $\tfrac{57}{5}\sqrt N$, but the padded call cost is then at most
$\sqrt N$. A regime term carried through both bounds cancels in their product.
The batch mean-square error is then at most
$\tfrac{637}{10}=1+\tfrac{57}{5}\cdot\tfrac{11}{2}$ times the squared modulus,
and $\sqrt{(9/2)\cdot637/10}<17$
([record](../results/upper-disagreement-20261119/README.md)).

The constant $15$ sharpens the call second moment below the cap scale. If the
cap-$n$ classifier runs to its cap, the cap-$2n$ classifier agrees with it
unless the majority votes after $n$ and $2n$ flips differ. With $N_1$ and $N_2$
the successes in the two halves and $n=2m$, the votes differ only if
$m<N_1$ and $N_2<m$, or $N_1\le m$ and $m<N_2$. The halves are independent and
have the same law, so with $a=\Pr(m<N_1)$ this has probability at most
$a(1-a)+(1-a)a\le\tfrac12$, for every coin. Otherwise the smaller classifier
stopped early, which its drift and error terms bound. Below $x=3/4$ the drift
bounds are evaluated in five steps of $x$, and with the cap terms above the
split the weighted grid sum is at most $247/100$. So $d$ times the call second
moment is at most $(2+\sqrt2)\cdot\tfrac{247}{100}+\tfrac{41}{100}\le\tfrac{89}{10}$.
With the regime term the batch mean-square error is at most
$\tfrac{999}{20}=1+\tfrac{89}{10}\cdot\tfrac{11}{2}$ times the squared modulus,
and $\sqrt{(9/2)\cdot999/20}<15$
([record](../results/upper-sign-20261119/README.md)).

The constant $13$ uses the exact sign-change probability. With $n$ flips in
each block, the votes change exactly on $\{n<2N_1,\ N_1+N_2\le n\}\cup
\{2N_1\le n,\ n<N_1+N_2\}$. The joint law of $(N_1,N_2)$ is invariant under
the swap of the two blocks, the change and its swap are disjoint, and together
they are the event that exactly one of $N_1$ and $N_2$ exceeds $n/2$. So the
votes change with probability exactly $a(1-a)\le\tfrac14$, for every coin and
every $n$, the first level included. With the split moved to $x=19/20$ and
four bands of the first grid point, each with its own drift table, the
weighted grid sum is at most $89/50$, and $d$ times the call second moment is
at most $(2+\sqrt2)\cdot\tfrac{89}{50}+\tfrac{41}{100}\le\tfrac{13}{2}$. With
the regime term $129/25$ the batch mean-square error is at most
$\tfrac{147}{4}=1+\tfrac{13}{2}\cdot\tfrac{11}{2}$ times the squared modulus,
and $\sqrt{(9/2)\cdot147/4}<13$
([record](../results/upper-quarter-20261119/README.md)).

The constant $12$ sharpens the padded call cost and drops the two-level
mixture. The cost sum groups its survival terms by the difference $j+1$ of
the level and the check, one dyadic grid each with threshold scale
$v_j=1+(j+1)\log2$. The offset bound of these grids decreases with $v_j$:
it is at most $399/100$ for $j\ge2$, in place of $17/4$. With the square
roots of the first eight scales bounded one by one, the padded call cost is at
most $\tfrac{26}{5}\min(\sqrt N,1/d)$, in place of $\tfrac{11}{2}$. The
batch machine of the frozen construction marks a call of cost $C$ when its
uniform seed mark $u$ satisfies $u<C/2^L$. Run on a seed whose marks are
multiplied by $t\ge1$, the same machine marks it when $u<C/(t2^L)$, so it
realizes the calibration at scale $t2^L$, which need not be a power of two,
with the same calls. The family runs level $L$ at scale $B/4$, with
$t=B/(4\cdot2^L)\in[1,2)$, and uses the whole budget. Its mean-square error
is at most $(1+\tfrac{169}{5t})\,\omega(2^{-L/2})^2\le4(t+\tfrac{169}{5})\,
\omega(B^{-1/2})^2\le\tfrac{716}{5}\,\omega(B^{-1/2})^2$, and
$\sqrt{716/5}<12$. The two-level mixture with the same bounds would give
$\sqrt{(9/2)\cdot174/5}\approx12.5$
([record](../results/upper-scale-20261119/README.md)).

A [numerical study](../research/2026-11-19-upper-constant-12/README.md)
computes the risk of this family. Its worst ratio
$\mathrm{RMS}/\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2})$ is $5.07$ at $N=2^{14}$ and tends to
about $5.37$ as $B$ grows; that of the single-level estimator is near $7.6$
([earlier study](../research/2026-11-18-upper-constant/README.md); its
point-mass bound, $R_{\delta_d}(B)\le\omega_{\delta_d}(B^{-1/2})/2$ for a known point mass and
$B\ge1$, is Lean-checked as [`point_mass_half`](../lean/ThresholdHC/PaperStatements/Full.lean)). In the
large-budget limit, the exact one-coin constants are about $2.2$ for the
call second moment and $3.6$ for the call cost, against $13/2$ and $26/5$
here. The Hoeffding bounds that the Lean certificates round give about $6.0$
and $4.9$. Below the split the disagreement bound is one quarter plus the
drift terms, about $0.3$ to $0.6$. The calibration at scale $B/4$ costs the
factor $4$, in the actual risk as well; the bias stays that of the dyadic
level $L$, and its square is at most $t\le2$ times the squared modulus at
scale $B/4$. With the
paper's thresholds, the Lean method gives at best about $11.2$ with that
bias bound. A [scoping study](../research/2026-11-19-one-coin-bounds/README.md)
locates most of that loss in Hoeffding's inequality: Bahadur's binomial tail

The lower constant of the RMS law is sharp. For every gap law and every $B\ge1$,

$$R_\mu(B)\ \ge\ \tfrac16\,\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2}),$$

and $1/6$ is the largest constant valid for all gap laws and budgets: four
fresh flips attain equality at $B=4$ and $\mu=\delta_{1/2}$. Equality needs
$B=4$: for every law and every $B\ge1$ other than four the bound is strict,
also under both oracles, as Lean checks since 21 November 2026
([record](../results/sharp-strict-20261121/README.md)). For $B\ge25$ the
constant improves to $\sqrt{59/2100}\approx0.1676$, and further to $0.175198$
(below). The experiment is the
original one: arbitrary measurable private randomness, interleaving,
revisits and unbounded stopping under the uniform expected-work budget.
These statements are Lean-checked in the audited library
([specification](../lean/ThresholdHC/SharpRMS/Specification.lean),
[theorems](../lean/ThresholdHC/SharpRMS/Full.lean),
[audit record](../results/sharp-rms-20261117/README.md)); CI runs their
audit, `tools/verify_sharp_rms.py`. Appendix AA of the v6 paper proves them

The proof first reduces any gap law to a single gap: for every gap $g$,
$R_\mu(B)\ge\omega_\mu(g)\,R_{\delta_g}(B)$, by simulating each requested coin
from a coin of gap $g$ at the same cost. At the gap $g=1/\sqrt B$ it then
uses three arguments. For $B\le4$, a Bayes bound under the uniform prior.
For $4<B<25$, a weighted score inequality with the exact one-coin
information price, whose integral is bounded analytically near $B=4$ and by
an integer certificate over 140 bias intervals elsewhere; the Lean kernel
evaluates that certificate. For $B\ge25$, three sign biases compared under
one reference law. Appendix AA of the v6 paper and the
[research note](../research/2026-11-17-rms-constants/sharp-unrestricted.pdf)
give the written proof. The upper constant is $12$ (below). The coefficient is
also exactly $1/6$ in the restricted settings of the
[rms-gap note](../research/2026-11-17-rms-constants/README.md): budgets through
four, both oracles there, and protocols with at most three flips per coin.
Lean checks these as corollaries since 21 November 2026, and covers the note's
two unlimited-revisit classes only conditionally
([record](../results/restricted-rms-20261121/README.md)).

This is the constant that §5 of the paper proves. `sharp_lower` above replaces it by the optimal $1/6$ in the same model, which the next paragraph describes.

The [algorithm model](../lean/ThresholdHC/Tier6/AlgorithmModel.lean) takes the infimum over all measurable random-seed spaces and adaptive estimators, with expected work uniformly bounded over every measurable sign law. It permits unrestricted natural-number coin creation, revisits, interleaving, and unbounded stopping. The iid hidden magnitude environment is independent of algorithm randomness and is not visible to policy or output. Squared error and RMS use extended-real values, retaining infinite risk. `process_realization` constructs the infinite probability laws and proves their cylinder identities; realization, KL, absolute continuity, and testing bounds are not assumptions of the final minimax theorem. An immediate-stopping estimator proves the class nonempty and its minimax RMS risk at most one. See the [exact specification](../lean/ThresholdHC/Tier6/Specification.lean), [complete audit](../results/tier6_status.json), and [verification record](TIER6.md). The coefficient $1/64$ is not optimal; the sharp value is $1/6$.

[Theorem 2 and §6](../paper/paper.tex#L272) quantify over every measurable sign function $q$ at a fixed $\mu$. Put $I=\mathbb E_\mu[D^{-1}]$, $V=\sup_q\operatorname{Var}_{\mu,q}(Y)$, and $\overline W=\sup_q\mathbb E_{\mu,q}W$. An estimator unbiased for all such $q$ can have $V,\overline W<\infty$ exactly when $I<\infty$, and every such estimator satisfies $V\overline W\ge I^2/16$. These are uniform bounds, not a lower bound for each individual population. The fixed-gap example attaining the lower coefficient is in §6, equation `eq:fixed-gap-sharp`; it does not identify the optimum for every nondegenerate law. Lean checks the conclusion of the example: at every fixed gap $d$ some unbiased estimator has expected work at most one and largest variance exactly $1/(16d^2)$ (the proof uses one fresh observation), so the infimum of $V\overline W$ is $I^2/16$ at every point mass, and a bound $V\overline W\ge cI^2$ holds for every law with $I<\infty$ exactly when $c\le1/16$ ([`one_flip`, `unbiased_fixed_gap_equality`, `unbiased_sharp_coefficient`](../lean/ThresholdHC/PaperStatements/Full.lean), [record](../results/paper-statements-20261120/README.md)). Proposition `prop:bounded` is Lean-checked in both sentences, in the hidden-magnitude observable model: if $\mu((0,1/2))>0$, no algorithm that stops almost surely with almost surely nonnegative output under every sign law is unbiased ([`nonnegative_unbiased_impossible`](../lean/ThresholdHC/NonnegativeUnbiased/Full.lean), [record](../results/nonnegative-unbiased-20261121/README.md)), while at the gap $1/2$ an unbiased $[0,1]$-valued estimator with work at most one exists (`half_gap_one_flip`).

The converse is now proved in Lean: [`unbiased_converse`](../lean/ThresholdHC/UnbiasedConverse/Full.lean)
inhabits the [full specification](../lean/ThresholdHC/UnbiasedConverse/Specification.lean)
in the unchanged Tier 6 observable adaptive experiment. For every measurable
seed space and estimator, unbiasedness for every measurable sign law and finite
uniform bounds on extended variance and actual expected flips imply inverse-gap
integrability and the product bound with coefficient 1/16. Unbiasedness explicitly
requires integrability; the conclusion does not assume a finite inverse moment.
No information bound, differentiability, bounded horizon, or finite coin supply
is an assumption of the final theorem.

The proof derives symmetric information bounds for vanishing clipped tilts,
obtains `modulus μ h ^ 2 ≤ 16 * h ^ 2 * W * V`, then applies monotone convergence
to truncated inverse gaps. The [audit](../results/unbiased_converse_001.json)
checks the exact target, all 10 modules and 38 declaration roots, transitive
axioms, and source stability. This proves the necessity direction and lower
coefficient in the original model. The construction below now supplies the
sufficiency direction in that same experiment. Their combination is the exact
Lean theorem `observable_unbiased_iff` in the hidden-magnitude model. The
oracle results below concern RMS and confidence, not an extended unbiased converse.

## Magnitude-oracle lower bounds

The [sharp coefficient](#minimax-law-and-unbiasedness) of the RMS law holds
for both oracles as well. For either oracle and every $B\ge1$,
$R_\mu^{\mathrm{oracle}}(B)\ge\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2})/6$, with equality at $B=4$
and $\mu=\delta_{1/2}$, and the constant improves to $\sqrt{59/2100}$ for
$B\ge25$. The reduction to one gap allows an arbitrary measurable array of
magnitudes conditional on the oracle seed, which covers free inspection of
the whole iid array and a magnitude selected at each creation.
[Lean theorems](../lean/ThresholdHC/SharpRMS/Full.lean) ·
[oracle proofs](../lean/ThresholdHC/SharpRMS/OracleAttainment.lean).
The stronger [lower bounds for budgets of at least 25](#minimax-law-and-unbiasedness),
$0.175198$ and $0.17807955$ for $B\ge100$, hold for both oracles too.

The rms-constants package also states the oracle upper bound
$R_\mu^{\mathrm{oracle}}(B)\le\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2})/2$ for $B\ge1$, with $1/2$
sharp. Since 21 November 2026 Lean checks the sharpness half: for every
$B\ge1$ and $c<1/2$, point masses at all sufficiently small gaps have both
oracle risks above $c\,\omega(B^{-1/2})$, so no oracle upper coefficient below
$1/2$ holds ([record](../results/oracle-sharpness-20261121/README.md)). The
upper bound is Lean-checked as well since the same date, for every gap law and
every $B\ge1$, with the floor form at the scale $1/(2\sqrt{\lfloor B\rfloor})$,
so $1/2$ is exactly the best universal oracle coefficient
([record](../results/oracle-upper-20261121/README.md)).

The original oracle theorems are frozen targets, Lean-checked for both
revealed and adaptively selected magnitudes. They prove the RMS bound with
the constant $1/64$, which the sharp $1/6$ above supersedes, and the
confidence bound with $15/(256\sqrt2)$, which is still the checked
confidence coefficient. For either oracle,

$$R_\mu^{\mathrm{oracle}}(B)\ge\frac{\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2})}{64},\qquad
r_\mu^{\mathrm{oracle}}(B,\delta)\ge
\frac{15}{256\sqrt2}\,\omega_\mu\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{B}}\right)$$

for $B\ge1$ and $0<\delta\le1/4$. The [full theorems](../lean/ThresholdHC/OracleLower/Full.lean)
include algorithm-level bounds, minimax bounds, and nonvacuity. Work is the
extended-real expectation of actual flips, and all measurable sign laws are
allowed. In the selected experiment, $q$ is defined on the entire gap domain,
even outside the support of the target law $\mu$.

The [model](../lean/ThresholdHC/OracleLower/Model.lean) gives the revealed
algorithm the entire iid magnitude array for free, a stronger grant than
revealing gaps only when coins are created. The selected interface explicitly
creates a coin at a magnitude chosen from the seed and previous flips,
revisits a previously created coin, or stops. Its
[semantic theorem](../lean/ThresholdHC/OracleLower/SelectedSemantics.lean)
proves that creation uses an unused label and revisits preserve the birth
magnitude. Counterfactual histories supply a countable array of potential
coins; they do not restrict magnitudes to a fixed deterministic grid.
The canonical infinite experiment is proved to realize the explicit finite
transcript laws. No KL bound, realization identity, or stopping assumption
is a premise of the final lower bounds. Finite uniform work implies
almost-sure stopping.

Fix a finite set of $m$ coins with magnitudes $0<d_i\le1/2$. The hypotheses have sign probabilities $q_\pm(i)=1/2\pm\eta_i$, where $0\le\eta_i\le1/8$, $h>0$, and $d_i\eta_i\le h/8$. Policies adapt to observation history. Write $T_n$ for actual work by finite horizon $n$.

- `ThresholdHC.Proofs.stopped_transcript_kl` in [Transcript.lean](../lean/ThresholdHC/Proofs/Tier2/Transcript.lean) proves $\operatorname{KL}(\mathsf P_{+,n},\mathsf P_{-,n})\le(128/225)h^2\mathbb E_+T_n$ for the explicit laws in [Statements2.lean](../lean/ThresholdHC/Statements2.lean).
- `ThresholdHC.Tier3.randomized_information_bound` in [RandomizedBase.lean](../lean/ThresholdHC/Tier3/RandomizedBase.lean) proves the average conditional information bound for an arbitrary common measurable random seed. `ThresholdHC.Tier3.randomized_marginal_kl_bound` and `ThresholdHC.Tier3.randomized_testing_bound` in [Randomized.lean](../lean/ThresholdHC/Tier3/Randomized.lean) handle the seed-marginal transcript and tests allowed to observe the seed. - `ThresholdHC.Tier3.unbounded_stopping` in [StoppingBase.lean](../lean/ThresholdHC/Tier3/StoppingBase.lean) derives almost-sure halting under both hypotheses from finite expected actual work under the plus hypothesis alone. `ThresholdHC.Tier3.unbounded_testing` in [Stopping.lean](../lean/ThresholdHC/Tier3/Stopping.lean) proves $\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)\le(128/225)h^2\mathbb E_+T$ for $0<\delta<1/2$ when both terminal-test errors are at most $\delta$. - `ThresholdHC.Tier3.unbounded_example` in [Examples.lean](../lean/ThresholdHC/Tier3/Examples.lean) supplies a geometric-seed experiment with finite expected work and positive probability of exceeding every finite cap.

## Rare-event optimizer

For any magnitude law $\mu$ on $(0,1/2]$, $0<\rho<1$, and $h>0$, define

$$\Psi_{\mu,\rho}(h)=\sup_{\substack{v\text{ measurable},\ 0\le v\le1\\\mathbb E_\mu v(D)\le\rho}}\mathbb E_\mu\min\{v(D),h\sqrt{v(D)}/D\}.$$

`ThresholdHC.Tier3.arbitrary_law_cutoff` and `ThresholdHC.Tier3.arbitrary_law_optimizer` in [Optimizer.lean](../lean/ThresholdHC/Tier3/Optimizer.lean) prove a unique $t\in(0,1/2)$ satisfies $\mathbb E_\mu\min\{1,t^2/D^2\}=\rho$, and $v_*(d)=\min\{1,t^2/d^2\}$ is an optimizer for every $h>0$. They prove saturation at $\rho$ for $h\ge t$ and, for $h<t$,

$$\Psi_{\mu,\rho}(h)=\Pr(D\le h)+h\mathbb E[D^{-1};h<D\le t]+ht\mathbb E[D^{-2};D>t].$$

The [specification](../lean/ThresholdHC/Tier3/OptimizerModel.lean) permits atoms and singular laws and assumes no finite inverse moment. It formalizes the identity in [continuation §3.3](../inputs/next-phase/report/research_note.tex#L218). The proposed matching arbitrary-law statistical upper bound is refuted by the Lean-checked critical-law counterexample below; the variational identity remains valid. The matching rare-event construction in the preserved continuation's §4 covers power margins $F(d)=(d/a)^\alpha$ on $(0,a]$ for $0<\alpha<2$, not $\alpha\ge2$.

**The statistical lower bound is Lean-checked.** For every probability law on
$(0,1/2]$, every $\rho$ and every $B>0$,
[`psi_le_selection`](../lean/ThresholdHC/PsiSelection/Full.lean) proves the
deterministic comparison

$$\Psi_{\mu,\rho}(B^{-1/2})\le\mathcal K_{\mu,\rho}(B),$$

where $\mathcal K$ is the entropy-tail selection functional of the
[next section](#general-law-rare-event-characterization). The proof splits the
$\Psi$ integrand over a submeasure $\sigma\le\mu$ and its complement: the
complement pays at most the truncated inverse-gap cost, and Cauchy–Schwarz
bounds the selected part by $h\sqrt{\rho\Gamma_\sigma}$. With the checked
explicit entropy-tail bounds, `psi_risk_lower` and `psi_confidence_lower` give,
for $0<\rho\le1/4$, $B>0$ and $0<\delta\le1/4$,

$$10^{-17}\Psi_{\mu,\rho}(B^{-1/2})\le R_{\mu,\rho}(B),\qquad
10^{-17}\Psi_{\mu,\rho}\bigl(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/B}\bigr)\le r^{\rm exp}_{\mu,\rho}(B,\delta).$$

Here $r^{\rm exp}$ is the minimax confidence radius at failure probability
$\delta$ under a uniform expected budget. These are the two halves of v6
Theorem A.5, with an explicit coefficient and every positive budget. The paper proof in Appendix A imports the single-coin
Hellinger bound of Lee and Valiant; the Lean route does not, and the paper
proof is kept unchanged. The specification was written with the proof.

## General-law rare-event characterization

**Current statement.** For every probability law $\mu$ on $(0,1/2]$, every
known rarity $0<\rho\le1/4$ and every $B>0$, Lean checks

$$10^{-17}\,\mathcal K_{\mu,\rho}(B)\le R_{\mu,\rho}(B)\le28000\,\mathcal K_{\mu,\rho}(B),$$

([statement](../lean/ThresholdHC/EntropyTailSharp/Specification.lean)). The

is charged under a uniform expected-work budget. Creation, revisiting,
interleaving, nonlinear outputs, and unbounded stopping are allowed. The upper

With $h=B^{-1/2}$, its entropy-tail selection functional is

$$\mathcal K_{\mu,\rho}(B)=\min\left\{\rho,\inf_{0\le\sigma\le\mu}
\left[h\sqrt{\rho\Gamma_\sigma}

in canonical dyadic bands, defined by increasing finite prefixes in Section
3.1 of the [source](../paper/rare-event/v2/sections/additive.tex). Section 4.6
records conservative universal constants:

$$10^{-17}\mathcal K_{\mu,\rho}(B)\le R_{\mu,\rho}(B)
\le33000\mathcal K_{\mu,\rho}(B).$$

No finite inverse moment, density, or regularity assumption is imposed.
The [explicit comparison](../lean/ThresholdHC/EntropyTailSharp/Full.lean) now
proves this lower bound and the stronger upper coefficient $28000$ in Lean.

## Rare-event power margins above two

The [full observable Lean package](../lean/ThresholdHC/RareAboveTwo/Full.lean) now proves the rate first established in the separate [27 October research note](../research/2026-10-27-route-a/construction.tex):

$$R_{\mu,\rho}(B)\asymp_\alpha\min\{\rho,\sqrt\rho/(a\sqrt B)\},\qquad \alpha>2,$$

for $F_\mu(d)=(d/a)^\alpha$ on $(0,a]$, all $0<a\le1/2$, $0<\rho\le1/4$, $B\ge1$, and every measurable $q$ with $\int q\,d\mu\le\rho$. The upper algorithm observes only coin flips and an independent seed. It knows $a,\rho$ (and $\alpha,B$ for budget calibration), and its upper guarantee also holds uniformly over laws with $F_\mu(d)\le(d/a)^\alpha$ for $d<a$, without knowing the law itself.

The original paper construction uses asymmetric sign-test errors and a randomized telescope. Its unbiased single-coin output has $\mathbb E Y^2\le(153/56)\rho$ and separately $\mathbb E T\le w_\alpha/a^2$, with explicit finite $w_\alpha$ in the note. The central integration step is $\mathbb E[q(D)\min(D,a)^{-2}]\le[\alpha/(\alpha-2)]a^{-2}\rho^{1-2/\alpha}$; this uses the global constraint, not $q(d)\le\rho$. Compound-Poisson replication enforces the exact expected budget, and a constant estimate covers saturation. The matching lower proof imports Lee–Valiant Corollary 19 and Lemma 18, then gives an explicit fixed-gap simulation reduction.

The [adversarial review](../research/2026-10-27-route-a/review.md), [second analytic review](../research/2026-10-27-route-a/second-review.md), [primary-source comparison](../research/2026-10-27-route-a/sources.md), and [baseline ledger](../research/2026-10-27-route-a/BASELINE.md) record the evidence. Reviewers did not author the main proof; model diversity is not guaranteed. The new Lean construction has second moment at most $28\rho$ and uses fixed-count averaging with the constant saturation estimator. Its matching lower coefficient is $1/256$ for every law supported below $a$, proved without importing an external statistical theorem. These two constants are instantiated in the proofs; the frozen statements assert positive constants, and the later exact targets `rare_single_coin_28`, `rare_lower_256` and `rare_minimax_256` state them ([record](../results/explicit-constants-20261121/README.md)). The [formal scope and proof map](RARE_ABOVE_TWO.md) distinguish these conservative constants and the formal algorithm from the paper construction. The seven frozen targets also cover exact-law existence, Psi equivalence and nonvacuity. External specialist review and worldwide novelty remain unverified. At the 27 October paper checkpoint, the arbitrary-law conjecture and $\alpha=2$ were unresolved. The later critical-law theorem below resolves the specified quadratic law and refutes the matching-Psi conjecture. The [general-law manuscript](#general-law-rare-event-characterization) supplies an entropy-tail replacement as an audited paper proof, since Lean-checked in both directions with conservative universal constants. The separate finite-gap manuscript below gives a closed quantile formula for known finite laws, including two atoms; infinite-support and unknown-law guarantees remain outside that manuscript's scope. Constants diverge as $\alpha\downarrow2$. The preserved paper and imported continuation are unchanged.

### The rate in closed form

Theorem 1.1 of the [standalone manuscript](../paper/finite-gap-rare-events/paper.pdf)
states, for every known finite positive-gap law, $0<\rho\le1/4$ and $B>0$,

$$10^{-20}Q(B)\le R_{\mu,\rho}(B)\le30001Q(B),\qquad Q(B)=\min\{\rho,T_h+h\sqrt{\rho\Gamma_\kappa}\},\quad h=B^{-1/2}.$$

Here $\nu$ is exactly the smallest-gap mass $\rho$, $\kappa=\mu-\nu$,
$T_h=\int\min\{1,h/d\}\,\nu(dd)$, and $\Gamma_\kappa$ is the dyadic
inverse-square cost entropy defined in the [paper guide](../paper/finite-gap-rare-events/README.md).
Rarity is the global constraint $\int q\,d\mu\le\rho$, with $0\le q\le1$.
Coins have iid complete persistent latent pairs; algorithms observe only
their independent seed, coin identities and purchased flips. Both bounds
use uniform expected physical-observation budgets and allow adaptive
creation, revisiting and interleaving.

### Computing the selection functional

**Lean verifies the finite-law bandwise-suffix theorem.** For a probability
law with finite support, a hard restriction minimizes the raw objective over
all submeasures and attains $K$ after clipping at $\rho$. If a larger atom in
the same band is skipped, moving equal inverse-square cost to that atom keeps
the entropy fixed and strictly increases the saved tail cost. A suffix is
determined by its selected count in each band, so at most $\prod_j(n_j+1)$
candidates suffice. [Theorem 5 and verification](VERIFY.md#finite-law-bandwise-suffix-theorem)

The suffix structure holds within each band, not across bands. In the
rare-event paper's example, masses $(1-\varepsilon)/2$, $\varepsilon$,
$(1-\varepsilon)/2$ at $1/2$, $1/4$, $1/8$ with $\varepsilon=1/1024$, $\rho=1/4$
and $h=1/1024$, the unique optimal hard selection retains $\{1/2,1/8\}$, so
no interval of gaps is optimal. Since 21 November 2026 Lean checks the
example: all eight enclosures of the paper's table, the strict minimum, the
inactive cap, and that every interval retention costs strictly more
([record](../results/noninterval-selection-20261121/README.md)).

**Lean verifies the mathematical cost-grouping algorithm.** The
[finite-menu audit](../results/band-cost-formal-20261105/README.md) checks
feasible compression, preservation of a global minimum, the
$\binom{m+r}{r}$ state bound and transition recurrence, entropy symmetry,
exact saving comparisons, and interval certificates. A further
[13-statement audit](../results/gap-law-menus-20261105/README.md) verifies the
concrete finite-law menu construction, its exact entropy and saving identities,
atomic formulas, and the equality of its optimized value with the original
$K$. Python execution remains unverified. Efficient exact selection with
unrestricted distinct band costs remains open.

**Prescribed relative accuracy is Lean-checked for the mathematical finite scheme.**

at most $\lfloor B\rfloor$ observations on every path. Such algorithms also meet
the expected budget, so $R(B)\le R^{\rm hard}(B)$ and every expected-budget
lower bound carries over unchanged.

**Reduction.** Let $\theta\in[0,M]$ with $0<M\le1$. Suppose that for every
dyadic $b\ge1$ an observable procedure has $\mathbb E T_b\le b$ and RMS at most
$Cf(b)$ uniformly over the parameter class, where $C\ge1/2$, $f(1/2)=M$ and

independent copy at $b=2^j$, stops it after $4\cdot2^j$ observations, and keeps
the last completed estimate, clipped to $[0,M]$. The estimate starts at $M/2$
and stays there if every stage times out; the constant below uses this
starting value. At $B\ge4$ the controller runs
every stage whose cap still fits in $\lfloor B\rfloor$ and has RMS at most
$\sqrt{32}\,Cf(B)$. At $B<4$ it returns $M/2$ without observing. Markov's
inequality bounds each timeout probability by $1/4$. No independence between a
run's error and its runtime is assumed; independence *between* complete runs is
essential.

## Earlier critical power margin: additive-class obstruction

At the single fixed law $F(d)=(4d)^2$, the new
[critical-regime note](../research/2026-10-27-critical-two/construction.tex)
proves, with universal constants and $L=1+\log(1/\rho)$,

$$R_{\rm add}(B)\asymp\min\{\rho,\sqrt{\rho L(1+\log L)/B}\}.$$

This restricted minimax class permits known $\mu,\rho,B$, a common independent
seed, an almost-surely finite seed-preselected coin count with finite mean,
and heterogeneous per-coin policies with unbounded stopping. Conditional on
the seed, components use independent genuine coins and local randomness;
each policy sees only its own observations. The output is an additive sum
plus a seed-dependent constant, with the square-sum and uniform moment/work
conditions stated in the note. Feedback across coins, data-dependent counts
and nonlinear terminal postprocessing are not covered.

The core theorem gives $\inf_A V(A)W(A)\asymp\rho L(1+\log L)$ for all
single-coin estimators with uniform bias at most $\rho/64$, including exact
unbiasedness. A component-selection reduction proves the population-class
lower bound without requiring identical kernels or predetermined finite
stopping horizons. The upper reuses the already available
[critical construction](../research/2026-10-27-minimax-audit/theorem/critical.tex).

Status: **paper proof with two separate agent reviews**. The [checkpoint](../research/2026-10-27-critical-two/CHECKPOINT.md)
links the complete proof, exact source hashes, prior-art comparison and checks.
No new Lean statistical theorem, external review or novelty certification is
claimed. Since $\Psi(B^{-1/2})\asymp\min\{\rho,\sqrt{\rho L/B}\}$ at this law,
the new result rules out a uniform matching-Psi guarantee in the specified
additive class. The later full critical-law theorem above resolves the
nonlinear/interactive gap; this earlier checkpoint's restricted statement and
evidence are preserved.

## Unknown rarity and numerical evidence

The [unknown-rarity obstruction](process/TRACK_D.md#an-obstruction-to-completely-uniform-adaptation-to-unknown-rarity) concerns a budget-indexed estimator family independent of the rarity bound. ## Practical comparison with a fixed plan

The [toolkit](../toolkit/README.md) implements the algorithm of
`distribution_blind_upper` and a fixed plan: $n$ fresh coins, an odd number $m$
of flips each, and the share of coins with a majority of heads. Over every sign
law at a known gap law, the fixed plan's bias is at most the mean
misclassification probability and its variance at most $1/(4n)$. The toolkit
evaluates the analytic formulas with binomial tails, using floating-point
arithmetic. The continuous uniform law uses an analytic integral rather than
a finite grid. Since 21 November 2026 Lean checks the bound for the plan run in
the original adaptive experiment: expected work $n(2k+1)$ for $m=2k+1$, bias
at most $e_m$, extended variance at most $1/(4n)$, RMS at most
$\sqrt{e_m^2+1/(4n)}$ over all sign laws, and for uniform success rates
$e_m=\binom{2a}{a}/2^{2a+1}=1/(2a\,\mathrm B(a,1/2))$ with $a=k+1$, in
binomial-tail form ([record](../results/fixed-plan-20261121/README.md)). The
numerical evaluation remains subject to roundoff.

Take the ten-coin example law from the toolkit guide, with probabilities 0.9,
0.8, 0.7, 0.6, 0.55, 0.52, 0.45, 0.3, 0.2 and 0.1 drawn uniformly, and
$B=16{,}384$. The fixed plan with $m=169$ and $n=96$ has RMS error at most 0.0713
for every sign law, about $0.75\,\omega_\mu(B^{-1/2})$. On the listed signs, the
distribution-blind algorithm's simulated RMS error is 0.277, with a standard
error of about 0.012 over 200 runs. For RMS error 0.03, the planner finds a
fixed plan using 258,153 flips for every sign law. In simulation, the blind algorithm first
reaches 0.03 at budget 4,194,304 (0.0271 over 200 runs). That is an unconfirmed hit:
the planner's 95% upper confidence bound there is 0.183, and confirming 0.03 would need
at least 6,761 runs.

This is numerical evidence for one law and, for the blind algorithm, one sign
assignment. The fixed plan uses the gap law to choose $m$, as the
distribution-wise minimax benchmark permits; the blind algorithm does not know
$\mu$. The comparison does not affect the theorem or its coefficient 512. It
shows that the blind algorithm's practical constants are far from those of a
plan tuned to the law at these budgets. The planner searches a 1.25× grid of
odd flip counts and does not claim a global optimum: for example, 428 coins
with 599 flips each give another qualifying plan at 256,372 flips.
To reproduce the grid result from `toolkit/`, run
`python -m threshold_toolkit --rates 0.9,0.8,0.7,0.6,0.55,0.52,0.45,0.3,0.2,0.1`
with `--budget 16384` or `--target-error 0.03`. The seed defaults to 0.
